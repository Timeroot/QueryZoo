\(\mathrm{C}^1(f)\)

1-certificate complexity

Largest minimal certificate over 1-inputs; the nondeterministic query complexity.

also written: nondeterministic query complexity, C_1

Definition

$\mathrm{C}^1(f) = \max_{x \in f^{-1}(1)} \mathrm{C}_x(f)$.

Basic facts

  • $\mathrm{D}(f) \le \mathrm{C}^1(f)\,\mathrm{bs}(f)$ [BBCMdW01, Thm 11].

Symmetric functions (general) total \(\max\{\,\ell_j + n - r_j \;:\; f_j = 1\,\}\) putative Exactly. Write $[\ell_j, r_j]$ for the maximal interval of weights containing $j$ on which the slice pattern is constant. A certificate at an input of weight $j$ fixes $a$ ones and $b$ zeros, and the completions have every weight in $[a,\ n-b]$, so it is valid iff $a \ge \ell_j$ and $n-b \le r_j$; both are attainable, since $\ell_j \le j$ ones and $n - r_j \le n-j$ zeros are available. Hence $\mathrm{C}_x = \ell_j + n - r_j$, and $\mathrm{C}^1$ maximises over $1$-inputs. Checks: $\vee_n$ gives $1$, $\mathrm{THR}^n_k$ gives $k$, $\oplus_n$ gives $n$, \(\mathrm{EH}_n\) gives $n$.
EXACT-HALF total \(n\) putative A $1$-input has weight exactly $n/2$, and flipping any single unfixed coordinate leaves the middle slice, so a $1$-certificate must fix all $n$ coordinates. This is the value that makes $\mathrm{C} = n$.
ODD-MAX-BIT total \(\lfloor n/2 \rfloor + 1\) putative Attained at $e_1$: fix $x_1 = 1$ and zero the $\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ even positions above it.

Rubinstein's function total \(n = \sqrt N\) putative Certifying $1$ means exhibiting one firing block, and a firing block must be pinned entirely (two ones and $n-2$ zeros): $\mathrm{C}^1(f) = \mathrm{C}^1(g) = n$.
Ambainis-Sun function total \(3k+2\) putative A firing pattern is a $1$-certificate of size $3k+2$, and $\mathrm{C}^1 \ge \mathrm{s}^1 = 3k+2$.
AND composed with NOT-EXACT-HALF total \(n^2/2 + n\) putative To certify $f = 1$ every block must be certified $\ne n/2$, and the cheapest witness for one block exhibits $n/2+1$ equal bits. So $\mathrm{C}^1 = n(n/2+1) = \Theta(N)$, matching the recorded $\mathrm{C}$.
Meiburg's tournament-subcube function total \(\le 7149\cdot 14011^{\,d-1}\) putative A $1$-input of the base function lies in one of the subcubes, whose codimension is $7149$, so $\mathrm{C}^1 \le 7149$ at $d = 1$. Composing, $\mathrm{C}^1(F\circ g^m) \le \mathrm{C}^1(F)\,\mathrm{C}(g)$ -- the outer $1$-certificate fixes both $0$s and $1$s, so the inner cost is the two-sided $\mathrm{C}(g) = 14011^{\,d-1}$. No lower bound is available: nothing in the construction forces a $1$-certificate to be large. Meiburg26
Pointer functions total \(\widetilde\Theta(\sqrt n)\) putative Variant $f_{2m,m}$; the argument of the $\mathrm{C}^0$ line with the roles of the two sides exchanged.
Cheat-sheet functions total \(\widetilde\Theta(\sqrt n)\) putative Same squeeze as the $\mathrm{C}^0$ line with the two sides exchanged; the $1$-certificates are moreover unambiguous, which is what the $\mathrm{UC}_{\min}$ line records.
Unambiguous-DNF / Alon-Saks-Seymour function total \(\widetilde O(n)\) Squeeze: $\mathrm{C}_1\le\mathrm{UC}_1=\widetilde O(n)$. The recorded $\mathrm{C}_0$ is quadratically larger -- this is the unambiguous-DNF-vs-CNF-width statement of [BBGJK21, Thm 1], matching the folklore $\mathrm{C}_0\le\mathrm{UC}_1^2$.
Gilmer-Saks-Srinivasan functions total \(n\) putative By [GSS16, Prop. 31] $\mathrm{C}_1(f)=\mathrm{C}_1(g_n)$, and $\mathrm{C}_1(g_n)=n$: the $1$-inputs of $g_n$ are isolated points (pairwise distance $\ge n/100$), so every subcube containing one and no $0$-input is a single point. GSS16

Goos-Jayram-Pitassi-Watson function total \(\widetilde O(n^{1/3})\) The unambiguous decision tree costs $\Theta(k\log k)=\widetilde O(n^{1/3})$, so $\mathrm{C}_1\le\mathrm{UC}_1=\widetilde O(n^{1/3})$. GJPW18
Ambainis' exact-quantum function total \(\Theta(3^d)\) putative Certifying $\mathrm{NE}=1$ at the top means exhibiting two children with different values, so $\mathrm{C}_1(\mathrm{NE}^d)=\mathrm{C}_1(\mathrm{NE}^{d-1})+\mathrm{C}_0(\mathrm{NE}^{d-1})=\mathrm{C}_1(\mathrm{NE}^{d-1})+3^{d-1}$, and hence $\mathrm{C}_1(\mathrm{NE}^d)=(3^d+1)/2$. Even the $1$-side of certificate complexity is within a factor $2$ of the input length.

Surjectivity total \(\Theta(n)\) putative A $1$-certificate names one preimage for each of the $R$ range values, which is $R\log_2 R = n/2$ bits at $R = N/2$, and that suffices.
$k$-distinctness total \(k\) putative Exhibit the $k$ positions carrying a repeated symbol. Counting a query as one symbol read, as throughout this entry and \(\mathrm{ED}_n\).
Triangle finding total \(3\) Name the three edges. This is the whole difficulty: with $N$ variables and $1$-certificates of size $K$, no positive-weight adversary can prove more than $\Omega(\sqrt{NK})$ [MSS07, §1], which for $N = m^2$, $K = 3$ is exactly the $\Omega(m)$ already on record. MSS07

Sink of a tournament total \(m-1\) To certify a sink you must exhibit all $m-1$ of its in-edges, and that suffices.
Scorpion graphs total \(3m-6\) putative The $3m-6$ touch pairs of a triple $(s,t,b)$ are a certificate: they pin $N(s)=\{t\}$, $N(t)=\{s,b\}$ and $N(b) = V\setminus\{s\}$, and the feet may then do anything. So $3m-6 = \mathrm{s}^1 \le \mathrm{bs}^1 \le \mathrm{C}^1 \le 3m-6$ and all three coincide -- the scorpion is $1$-certificate-tight, verified by brute force for $m \le 9$.
