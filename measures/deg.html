Measures › Polynomial degree
\(\deg(f)\)

Exact degree

Degree of the unique multilinear real polynomial that represents f exactly.

also written: Fourier degree, real degree, polynomial degree

Definition

Every $f:\{0,1\}^n\to\mathbb{R}$ has a unique multilinear representation $p(x)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}\hat f(S)\prod_{i\in S}x_i$; $\deg(f)=\max\{|S| : \hat f(S)\ne 0\}$. (Equivalently, in the $\pm 1$ Fourier basis, the largest $|S|$ with a nonzero Fourier coefficient.)

Basic facts

  • $\deg(f)\le \mathrm{D}(f)$; $\mathrm{D}(f) = O(\deg(f)^3)$ [Midrijanis04].
  • $\deg(f) \le \mathrm{s}(f)^2$ [Huang19]; in fact $\deg(f)\le\lambda(f)^2$.
  • $\deg(f) = O(\widetilde{\deg}(f)^2)$ [ABKRT21]; tight, e.g. $\mathrm{OR}_n$.
  • $\deg(f)\le 2\,\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{E}}(f)$; $\deg(f)=n-O(n^{0.548})$ for all non-constant symmetric $f$.
  • $\mathrm{bs}(f)\le 2\deg(f)^2$ [Nisan-Szegedy].

Threshold total \(n\) putative The top Fourier coefficient is $\sum_{j\ge k}(-1)^{n-j}\binom{n}{j} = (-1)^{n-k}\binom{n-1}{k-1} \ne 0$, so the multilinear polynomial has the full monomial $x_1\cdots x_n$ for every $1 \le k \le n$. Symmetric functions (general) total \(n - O(n^{0.548})\) What is proved: von zur Gathen--Roche show $\deg(f) \ge n - O(n^{0.548})$ for every non-constant symmetric $f$, and $\deg(f)\le n$ is trivial, so $\deg(f) = n - O(n^{0.548})$. Their exponent comes from prime gaps: $\deg(f) = n - O(g(n))$ where $g$ bounds the gap below $n$, so any improvement on prime gaps improves this line automatically, and the Riemann Hypothesis would give $n - O(\sqrt n\log n)$. Conjecturally the truth is $\deg(f) \in \{n, n-1\}$ for every non-constant symmetric $f$ -- i.e. $\deg(f) = n - O(1)$; it has been checked exhaustively for small $n$, and it holds for every $f$ whose jump structure is a single threshold (see \(\mathrm{THR}^n_k\), where $\deg = n$ exactly). vzGathenRoche97

Simon's problem partial \(O(n)\) putative Exponentially below $\mathrm{D}$. Let $z_{i,v}$ be the degree-$n$ conjunction of input bits asserting $g(i) = v$. Then $P = \sum_{i<j}\sum_v z_{i,v}z_{j,v}$ counts colliding pairs, so $P = 0$ on injective tables and $P = 2^{n-1}$ on two-to-one tables, and $2^{1-n}P$ represents $f$ exactly on the promise with degree $2n$ in the input bits. Beware: $P$ is wildly unbounded off the promise, so this does not bound $\widetilde{\deg}$, which is separately $O(n)$ from $2\mathrm{Q}$.
Deutsch-Jozsa partial \(2\) putative On the promise $f$ is the symmetric function $t \mapsto [t = N/2]$ of $t=|x|$ restricted to $t \in \{0, N/2, N\}$, and $(1 - (1-2t/N)^2)$ interpolates it. Degree $1$ is impossible since $p(0)=p(N)=0$ forces $p \equiv 0$. So $\mathrm{deg} = 2 = 2\,\mathrm{Q}_E$: Deutsch-Jozsa makes $\mathrm{deg} \le 2\mathrm{Q}_E$ tight.
Unique search partial \(1\) putative $p(x) = \sum_i x_i$ agrees with $f$ at every point of the domain: it is $0$ at $0^n$ and $1$ at each $e_i$. Off the promise it grows to $n$, which the partial-function definition of $\deg$ (agree on $\mathrm{Dom}(f)$, unbounded elsewhere) permits. $f$ is not constant, so $\deg = 1$ exactly.
Two-or-none search partial \(1\) putative $p(x) = \tfrac12\sum_i x_i$ is $0$ at $0^n$ and $1$ at every weight-$2$ point, so it agrees with $f$ on the whole domain. Off the promise it runs up to $n/2$, which the partial-function definition of $\deg$ permits.
Marked bit partial \(n\) putative Exactly, and the contrast with \(\mathrm{SEARCH}^{\le 1}_n\) ($\deg = 1$) and \(\mathrm{ApproxIndex}_k\) ($\deg = 2$) is the whole design. Upper: $1 - \prod_b (1-y_b)$. Lower: symmetrise an exact $p$ over the $n$ cells to $q(j_1,j_0)$, of no higher degree, and put $r(t) = q(t,0) - q(0,t)$; then $r(t) = 1$ for every integer $t \in [1,n]$ while $r(0) = 0$, so $r - 1$ has $n$ roots and is not identically zero. Counting the marks cannot work here because their number is not fixed.
Dense marked bit partial \(n/2+1\) putative Exactly. Upper: $\deg \le \mathrm{D} = n/2+1$. Lower: let $p$ be exact on $\mathrm{Dom}$ and symmetrise over the $n$ cells. Each cell takes one of three values, so Minsky--Papert symmetrisation [MinskyPapert69] gives a polynomial $P(a,c)$ of no higher total degree in the two counts $a = |y|$ and $c = |z|$. The domain says $P(a,0) = 1$ for every integer $a \in [n/2,\, n]$ and $P(0,c) = 0$ for every integer $c \in [n/2,\, n]$ -- that is $n/2+1$ points each. Suppose $\deg p \le n/2$. Then $u(a) := P(a,0)$ is a univariate of degree $\le n/2$ agreeing with the constant $1$ at $n/2+1$ points, so $u \equiv 1$; symmetrically $w(c) := P(0,c) \equiv 0$. But $u(0) = P(0,0) = w(0)$, so $1 = 0$. Hence $\deg p \ge n/2+1$. The contradiction lives at the all-blank array, the one point the promise omits: an exact polynomial has to commit to a value there and cannot commit to two. MinskyPapert69
Gap-Majority partial \(\Theta(n)\) putative Exact degree ignores the promise gap entirely. Symmetrising, an exactly representing polynomial gives a univariate $p$ with $p(t) = 0$ for all $\lfloor n/2-\delta n\rfloor + 1$ integers $t \le n/2-\delta n$, so $\deg \ge (1/2-\delta)n$; and $\deg \le \mathrm{D} = \Theta(n)$. Against $\widetilde{\deg} = \Theta(\min(n,1/\delta))$ this is the cleanest $\deg$-vs-$\widetilde{\deg}$ gap there is.
Gap-Majority at constant gap partial \(\Theta(n)\) putative Exact degree ignores the promise gap: symmetrising an exactly representing polynomial gives a univariate with $\Theta(n)$ forced roots. Against $\widetilde{\deg} = \Theta(1)$ this is the cleanest $\deg$-vs-$\widetilde{\deg}$ gap there is.
Parity-labelled FIND-1 partial \(1\) putative The linear polynomial $p(x) = (4/n)\sum_{i \text{ odd}} x_i$ is $1$ on every $1$-input and $0$ on every $0$-input of the promise. Off the promise it runs up to $2$, which is allowed for $\deg$ but not for $\widetilde{\deg}$ -- see the settings note at \(\deg(f)\).
Approximate index partial \(\Theta(1)\) putative At most $2$, and at least $1$ since $f$ is not constant. Count the marked cells instead of finding one: the ball $B(a,r)$ has $V = \sum_{j \le r}\binom kj$ cells whatever the address $a$ is, and on the promise every one of them holds $v$ and no other cell does, so $\tfrac1V\sum_b \mathbb{1}[x_b = 1]$ equals $v$ exactly on the whole domain. Each $\mathbb{1}[x_b=1]$ is a degree-$\le 2$ function of that cell's two encoding bits, so $\deg \le 2$ -- and $\deg = 1$ under the encoding that spends one bit on $\mathbb{1}[x_b=1]$. The address is never looked at. Nothing survives this off the promise, where the sum runs up to $2^k \gg V$: it is unbounded, which is legal for $\deg$ (agree on $\mathrm{Dom}(f)$, unconstrained elsewhere) and is exactly what $\widetilde{\deg}$ forbids. So $\deg = \Theta(1)$ against $\mathrm{D} = \Theta(\sqrt{k\log k})$, and the unbounded polynomial method is worthless here; cf. \(\mathrm{SEARCH}^{\le 1}_n\), where the same collapse happens for the same reason.
Collision problem partial \(\Omega(n^{1/3})\) $\deg \ge \widetilde{\deg} = \Omega(n^{1/3})$, in the standard encoding of the table by indicator variables $x_{i,v}$.
Element distinctness total \(n\) putative Encode a symbol $x_i$ by the indicators $x_{i,v}$, one block per position; every function on $\Sigma^n$ is then multilinear of degree $\le n$, via $\sum_x f(x)\prod_i x_{i,x_i}$. For the matching lower bound restrict the domain to $x_i \in \{0, i\}$, where $0$ is a fixed symbol and the $i$ are pairwise distinct: writing $z_i = [x_i = 0]$, the input is all-distinct exactly when at most one $z_i$ is $1$, so $\mathrm{ED}_n$ restricts to $\lnot\mathrm{Th}^n_2$ on a Boolean cube of full dimension. That is a non-constant symmetric function whose univariate interpolant takes the values $1,1,0,\dots,0$, with leading coefficient proportional to $1 - n \ne 0$, hence degree exactly $n$. Restriction cannot raise degree, so $\deg(\mathrm{ED}_n) = n$: on the exact-degree scale there is no speedup at all, in sharp contrast with $\widetilde{\deg} = \Theta(n^{2/3})$.
Ambainis-Belovs function partial \(9\) Theorem 6, and the whole content of the upper bound. $K(x) = \sum_{a,b,c}\sum_{s,t} \mathbb{1}[x_a{=}s]\,\mathbb{1}[x_b{=}t]\,\mathbb{1}[x_c{=}r_{a,b,c}-s-t]$ has degree $3$ in the indicator variables $\mathbb{1}[x_i = v]$, and $T(z) = \tfrac14 z^3 - \tfrac32 z^2 + \tfrac94 z$ satisfies $T(0)=0$, $T(1)=1$ and $0 \le T \le 1$ on $[0,4]$, so $T(K(x)/n)$ represents $f$ exactly and is bounded on the whole cube. Degree $3 \times 3 = 9$, independent of $n$. AmbainisBelovs23
Surjectivity total \(n\) putative Full degree, for every power of two $R \ge 2$ and every even $N \ge R$ -- in particular at $R = N/2$. The coefficient of the top monomial $\prod_i x_i$ is $\pm\sum_x(-1)^{|x|}\mathrm{SURJ}(x)$. Inclusion-exclusion over the missed set gives $\sum_x(-1)^{|x|}\mathrm{SURJ}(x) = (-1)^N\sum_{S\subseteq[R]}(-1)^{|S|}\sigma(S)^N$ with $\sigma(S) = \sum_{v\in S}(-1)^{|v|}$, because $\sum_{v\in[R]}(-1)^{|v|} = 0$. Splitting $S$ by the parity of its elements and passing to exponential generating functions turns this into $N!\,[z^N]\big((1-e^z)(1-e^{-z})\big)^{R/2} = N!\,[z^N]\big(-4\sinh^2(z/2)\big)^{R/2}$, and expanding $\sinh$ leaves $\pm R!\,T(N,R)$, where $T$ is the central factorial number of the second kind. $T(N,R) > 0$ whenever $N \equiv R \bmod 2$ and $N \ge R$, which holds here since $R$ is even. Checked numerically at $R=2,N=4$ (coefficient $-2$) and $R=4,N=8$ (coefficient $504 = 4!\cdot T(8,4)$).
Ordered search partial \(1\) putative Under the zoo's partial-function convention -- agree with $f$ on the domain, unconstrained off it -- the promise makes $x_j = [\,j \ge k\,]$, so for any target $g(k)$ the degree-one polynomial $p(x) = g(0) + \sum_j (g(j+1)-g(j))(1 - x_j)$ is exactly right. Hence $\deg = 1$ for every Boolean predicate of $k$, and the unbounded polynomial method says nothing here. [BunVoronova23] write "degree $n$" for the same object; they mean a bounded polynomial.
Inner product mod 2 total \(n\) The multilinear extension is $\tfrac12\big(1 - \prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-2x_iy_i)\big)$, whose leading term $-\tfrac12(-2)^{m}\prod_i x_iy_i$ has degree $2m = n$ and does not cancel.
Minsky-Papert one-in-a-box total \(N = m^3\) Degree is multiplicative for block composition, and $\deg(\mathrm{AND}_m)\deg(\mathrm{OR}_{m^2}) = m\cdot m^2$.
Sink of a tournament total \(m-1 = \Theta(\sqrt N)\) Exactly: $\mathrm{SINK}_m = \sum_{v}\prod_{u\ne v}[u\to v]$, a sum of $m$ disjoint monomials of degree $m-1$, and the top-degree terms do not cancel. CMS19
Scorpion graphs total \(3m-6 = \Theta(\sqrt N)\) putative Exactly, for $m \ge 5$. For $m \ge 5$ a scorpion has a unique triple $(s,t,b)$ -- the body is the only vertex of degree $m-2$, since a foot misses both $s$ and $t$ -- so $\mathrm{SCORP}_m = \sum_{(s,t,b)} \mathbf{1}[\text{the } 3m-6 \text{ pairs touching } \{s,t,b\} \text{ take their scorpion values}]$ is a sum of disjoint subcube indicators of codimension $3m-6$. Hence $\deg \le 3m-6$. It is attained: all six orderings of one $3$-set share the same touch set $T$ and each contributes $(-1)^{2m-5} = -1$ to the coefficient of $\prod_{e\in T} x_e$, so that coefficient is $-6 \ne 0$. Confirmed exhaustively for $m \le 6$. Note $\deg = \mathrm{s}^1 = \mathrm{C}^1$, all three equal to $3m-6$.
Graph connectivity total \(\binom{m}{2} = N\) putative Full degree, so connectivity is evasive for the polynomial method too. The coefficient of the top monomial $\prod_e x_e$ in the multilinear extension is $\sum_{S}(-1)^{N-|S|}\mathrm{CONN}(S) = \pm\sum_{S \text{ connected}}(-1)^{|S|}$, and the classical alternating count of connected spanning subgraphs of $K_m$ is $(-1)^{m-1}(m-1)!$ -- never zero. Verified by exact expansion for $m \le 6$, where the top coefficient is $\pm 2, \pm 6, \pm 24, \pm 120$.
Ambainis–Iraids–Kokainis unambiguous DNF total \(O(n)\) A disjoint DNF equals the sum of its term indicators, each of degree ≤9n. AIK26
Pabbaraju’s optimal unambiguous DNF total \(O(n)\) Corollary 1.2: exact representation by the sum of disjoint term indicators. Pabbaraju26

Defined for

  • Partial functions yes
    Warning: for partial $f$ the representing polynomial is not unique, so $\deg(f)$ is defined as the minimum degree of a polynomial agreeing with $f$ on the domain (unbounded elsewhere). Most total-function relations involving $\deg$ break in this setting -- including $\widetilde{\deg} \le \deg$, which fails: on \(\mathrm{SEARCH}^{\le 1}_n\) $\deg = 1$ while $\widetilde{\deg} = \Theta(\sqrt n)$, and on \(\mathrm{OS}_n\) $\deg = 1$ while $\widetilde{\deg}$ grows. \(\widetilde{\deg}_{\varepsilon}(f)\) insists on boundedness at every point of the cube; $\deg$, \(\mathrm{ndeg}(f)\), \(\mathrm{rdeg}(f)\), \(\deg_p(f)\) and \(\widetilde{\deg}_{+}(f)\) constrain only $\mathrm{Dom}(f)$.
