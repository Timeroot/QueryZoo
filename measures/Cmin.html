\(\mathrm{C}_{\min}(f)\)

Minimum certificate complexity

Min (rather than max) over inputs of the smallest certificate.

Definition

$\mathrm{C}_{\min}(f) = \min_x \mathrm{C}_x(f)$. Appears in the study of "every function has a short certificate somewhere" and in the $\mathrm{UC}_{\min}$ variant used by Ben-David-Hatami-Tal.

OR total \(1\) Any $1$-input is certified by a single $1$.
AND total \(1\) Any $0$-input is certified by a single $0$.
PARITY total \(n\) Every input needs the whole assignment; PARITY is evasive in the strongest sense.
MAJORITY total \((n+1)/2\) Every input needs a full majority-sized certificate, so the min and the max coincide.
Threshold total \(\min\{k,\; n-k+1\}\) putative A $1$-input needs $k$ ones exhibited and a $0$-input needs $n-k+1$ zeros, so $\mathrm{C}(x)$ takes exactly the two values $k$ and $n-k+1$; the minimum over inputs is the smaller of them. Contrast $\mathrm{C} = \max\{k,\;n-k+1\}$: the gap between \(\mathrm{C}_{\min}(f)\) and \(\mathrm{C}(f)\) on this one family is already unbounded (take $k=1$).
Symmetric functions (general) total \(\min_j\,(\ell_j + n - r_j)\) putative The same per-input formula as the \(\mathrm{C}^1(f)\) line, minimised rather than maximised over all weights $j$. Equivalently $n$ minus the length of the longest constancy interval of the slice pattern, plus one. Checks: $\vee_n$ gives $1$, $\mathrm{THR}^n_k$ gives $\min\{k,\ n-k+1\}$, $\oplus_n$ gives $n$.
EXACT-HALF total \(n/2 + 1\) putative Every $0$-input costs $n/2+1$ and every $1$-input costs $n$, so the minimum is the $0$-side value. The factor-$2$ gap to $\mathrm{C} = n$ is the whole of the asymmetry of $\mathrm{EH}$.
ODD-MAX-BIT total \(1\) putative At $x = e_n$, reading $x_n = 1$ already decides (the answer is the parity of $n$). Certificate complexity varies by a factor of $\Theta(n)$ across inputs.
ADDRESS / multiplexer total \(k+1\) putative A certificate that fixes $j$ of the address bits leaves $2^{k-j}$ live addresses and must therefore pin all $2^{k-j}$ of the corresponding table entries, for a total of $j + 2^{k-j} \ge k+1$ coordinates, with equality only at $j=k$ (and $j=k-1$). So every input has $\mathrm{C}(x) = k+1$: $\mathrm{C} = \mathrm{C}_{\min} = k+1$ and $\mathrm{ADDR}$ is perfectly balanced in certificate size.
Binary tree navigation total \(k\) putative Every input has the same certificate structure.
TRIBES total \(\Theta(\log n)\) The cheapest input to certify is a $1$-input: one satisfied tribe, $w=\Theta(\log n)$ bits.
AND-OR tree (depth 2) total \(n\) putative Certifying a $1$ needs a witness in each of the $n$ blocks; certifying a $0$ needs a whole empty block, $n$ bits. So every input costs exactly $n$: $\mathrm{C} = \mathrm{C}_{\min} = n$.
NAND tree (balanced AND-OR formula) total \(\Theta(\sqrt N)\) putative A minimal certificate takes one child at every $0$-forcing gate and both children at every $1$-forcing gate (or vice versa), so it has $2^{\lceil d/2\rceil}$ or $2^{\lfloor d/2 \rfloor}$ leaves -- $\Theta(\sqrt N)$ either way. Every input of the balanced NAND tree costs the same up to $\sqrt2$: $\mathrm{C} = \Theta(\mathrm{C}_{\min})$.
Recursive MAJORITY-of-3 total \(2^d\) putative Every input of $\mathrm{MAJ}_3$ costs exactly $2$ to certify, so certificate complexity is the same at every input at every level: $\mathrm{C}_{\min} = \mathrm{C} = 2^d$. Recursive majority is perfectly homogeneous in this respect, unlike \(K^{\circ k}\) ($\mathrm{C}_{\min} = \mathrm{C}^{0.77}$).
Kushilevitz's function (iterated) total \(4^k = N^{0.7737}\) putative Exhaustive search over the $64$ inputs of the base function gives $\mathrm{C}_{\min}(K)=4$, attained on both sides ($\min_{K(x)=0}\mathrm{C}_x = \min_{K(x)=1}\mathrm{C}_x = 4$). Minimum certificate complexity composes: a certificate of $f\circ g^n$ at $x$ restricts to a certificate of $f$ at $g(x)$ on the blocks it certifies, so $\mathrm{C}_x(f\circ g^n) = \min_{S}\sum_{j\in S}\mathrm{C}_{x^j}(g)$ over certificates $S$ of $f$, and since $K$ is $4$ on both sides the minimum is $4\cdot\mathrm{C}_{\min}$ of the previous level. So $\mathrm{C}_{\min} = 4^k$ against $\mathrm{C} = 6^k$ -- a genuine gap, unlike \(\mathrm{AND}_n \circ \mathrm{OR}_n\) where the two coincide.
Rubinstein's function total \(n = \sqrt N\) putative The cheapest inputs are the $1$-inputs, at cost $\mathrm{C}^1 = n$; a $0$-input costs at least $2n$, since each of the $n$ blocks needs at least $\mathrm{C}_{\min}(g) = 2$ bits. So $\mathrm{C}_{\min} = \sqrt N$ against $\mathrm{C} = N/2$.
Ambainis-Sun function total \(3k+2\) putative The $1$-inputs are the cheap ones: $\mathrm{C}_{\min} = \mathrm{C}^1 = 3k+2 = \Theta(\sqrt N)$.
AND composed with NOT-EXACT-HALF total \(n = \sqrt N\) putative The $0$-inputs are far cheaper to certify than the $1$-inputs: $\mathrm{C}_{\min} = \mathrm{C}^0 = n = \sqrt N$ against $\mathrm{C} = \Theta(N)$.
Meiburg's tournament-subcube function total \(\le 7149\cdot 14011^{\,d-1}\) putative $\mathrm{C}_{\min} = \min\{\mathrm{C}^0,\mathrm{C}^1\} = \mathrm{C}^1$ here, since the \(\mathrm{C}^1(f)\) bound $7149\cdot 14011^{\,d-1} \approx 0.51\cdot 14011^{\,d}$ is strictly below the exact $\mathrm{C}^0 = 14011^{\,d}$. The relation $\mathrm{D} \le \mathrm{C}_{\min}\cdot\mathrm{C}$ therefore gives only $\mathrm{D} = O(14011^{2d})$, far above the recorded lower bound.
Pointer functions total \(\widetilde\Theta(\sqrt n)\) putative Variant $f_{2m,m}$, a $2m\times m$ grid with $n=\widetilde\Theta(m^2)$ bits. Upper: $\mathrm{C}_{\min}\le\mathrm{C}=\widetilde O(\sqrt n)$. So $\mathrm{C}_x\ge m=\widetilde\Omega(\sqrt n)$ for every input $x$, not just the worst one.

Ambainis' exact-quantum function total \(\Theta(2.3028^d)\) putative Exact recursion. Let $a_d=\min_{x\in f^{-1}(0)}\mathrm{C}_x$ and $b_d=\min_{x\in f^{-1}(1)}\mathrm{C}_x$. A $0$-certificate must pin all three children (they must be shown equal): $a_d=3\min\{a_{d-1},b_{d-1}\}$. A $1$-certificate need only exhibit two children with different values: $b_d=a_{d-1}+b_{d-1}$. With $a_0=b_0=1$ one gets $b_d<a_d$ for all $d\ge1$, hence $a_d=3b_{d-1}$ and $b_d=b_{d-1}+3b_{d-2}$, i.e. $1,2,5,11,26,59,137,\dots$, whose growth rate is the root $(1+\sqrt{13})/2=2.3027\ldots$ So $\mathrm{C}_{\min}=\Theta(2.3028^d)=\Theta(n^{0.7587})$, strictly between $\lambda=n^{1/2}$ and $\mathrm{C}=n$.
FORRELATION partial \(\Theta(n)\)· putative Every certificate, at every promise input, fixes $\Omega(n)$ coordinates. Suppose one fixes fewer than $10^{-3}n$; then at least $0.999n$ coordinates of $x$ and $0.999n$ of $y$ are free, and by Cauchy--Schwarz the fixed $y$-positions carry $\sum_{\text{fixed } j}|\hat x_j| \le \sqrt{10^{-3}n} \le 0.032\sqrt n$. At a $1$-input, $\Phi \ge 3/5$ forces $\sum_j |\hat x_j| \ge 0.6\sqrt n$; setting every free $y_j$ to $-\mathrm{sign}(\hat x_j)$ drives $\Phi$ below $-0.5$, and reverting those bits one at a time back to their original values moves $\Phi$ in steps of $2|\hat x_j|/\sqrt n \le 2/\sqrt n < 1/50$ up to $\Phi \ge 3/5$, so some intermediate assignment has $|\Phi| \le 1/100$ -- a $0$-input consistent with the certificate. At a $0$-input, set the free $x$-coordinates uniformly at random: then $\sum_j |\hat x_j| \ge 0.7\sqrt n$ with high probability, and setting every free $y_j$ to $\mathrm{sign}(\hat x_j)$ gives $\Phi \ge 0.7 - 2(0.032) > 3/5$, a $1$-input consistent with the certificate. Either way the set was not a certificate. With $\mathrm{C}_{\min} \le \mathrm{C} \le 2n$ this pins $\Theta(n)$.

Ambainis-Belovs function partial \(O(n)\) At most $\mathrm{C} = O(n)$, i.e. $\min\{\mathrm{C}^0,\mathrm{C}^1\} \le O(n)$.
Ben-David-Kundu total search problems relation \(\Theta(\log^6 n)\) putative Unusually, the min and the max coincide. Any certificate must name an output $(i_1,\dots,i_t)$ and a shift, and to know that $i_\ell$ is an all-$1$ block of $x^\ell \oplus h$ one must have read all $\log(n/2)$ bits of that block, for every $\ell$. So $\mathrm{C}_x \ge t\log(n/2)$ on every input, matching the upper bound of the \(\mathrm{C}(f)\) entry.
Yamakawa-Zhandry problem relation \(n\) putative Every input has the same $n$-bit certificate shape, so the minimum over inputs equals the maximum.
Surjectivity total \(\Theta(n/\log n)\) putative Upper: $\mathrm{C}_{\min} \le \mathrm{C}^0 = N$. Lower: in any certificate at most $R-1$ slots can be left entirely unfixed -- $R$ free slots could be set to $1,\dots,R$ and make the input surjective -- so every certificate touches at least $N-R+1 = R+1$ slots, and $\mathrm{C}_{\min} \ge R+1 = \Theta(N)$. The $1$-side is more expensive ($\mathrm{C}^1 = n/2$), so the minimum is attained on $0$-inputs.
$k$-distinctness total \(k\) putative The cheapest certificate anywhere is a $k$-fold repeat; every $0$-input costs $n$.
Triangle finding total \(3\) putative A $1$-certificate is a triangle and cannot be smaller; every $0$-input costs $\binom m2$.
Welded trees relation \(\Theta(1)\) $1\le\mathrm{C}_{\min}\le\mathrm{C}=\Theta(1)$: nine queries exhibit the EXIT vertex and certify it at every input, so the cheapest input is no cheaper than a constant.
Local search on the hypercube relation \(n+1\) Exactly $n+1$, like $\mathrm{C}$: certifying that a vertex is a local minimum means reading it and all $n$ neighbours, and that cost is the same at every input, so the min over inputs equals the max.
Local search on the grid relation \(\Theta(1)\) Between $d+1$ and $\mathrm{C}=2d+1$: a corner cell has only $d$ neighbours to check, an interior cell has $2d$, and $d$ is a constant.
Fourier fishing relation \(\Theta(N)\) putative Not just the worst input: every input is expensive. Certifying the answer $z$ means forcing $|\sum_x f(x)(-1)^{x\cdot z}|\ge\sqrt N$, and the unread cells can contribute $-(N-|S|)$ to that sum, so a certificate of size $|S|$ forces nothing unless $|S|\ge(N+\sqrt N)/2$. Hence $\mathrm{C}_{\min}\ge N/2$, matching $\mathrm{C}=\Theta(N)$.
Ordered search partial \(1\) putative At the two extreme inputs $0^n$ and $1^n$ a single query at the relevant end suffices, since only one promise input is consistent with it.
Tseitin search on an expander relation \(\Theta(1)\) putative At most $\mathrm{C} = O(1)$, at least $1$.
Inner product mod 2 total \(n/2\) putative A certificate must kill every term $x_iy_i$: for each $i$ it must fix $x_i=0$, or $y_i=0$, or both $x_i=y_i=1$. So $\mathrm{C}(x,y) = m + |x \wedge y|$ exactly. The minimum is $m = n/2$, attained at every input with $x \wedge y = 0$ (e.g. $x = 0^m$), and the maximum is $2m = n$ at $x=y=1^m$ -- which is where the recorded $\mathrm{C} = n$ comes from.
Minsky-Papert one-in-a-box total \(m = N^{1/3}\) putative Every $1$-input costs exactly $m$ (one witness per box) and every $0$-input costs exactly $m^2$ (a whole empty box), so the minimum is the $1$-side: $\mathrm{C}_{\min} = m$ against $\mathrm{C} = m^2$.
Sink of a tournament total \(m-1\) putative The cheapest inputs are the $1$-inputs, at $\mathrm{C}^1 = m-1$; every $0$-input costs $m$. So certificate complexity is essentially constant across SINK's domain, in sharp contrast to its sensitivity, which swings from $3$ to $m-1$.
Scorpion graphs total \(m-1\) putative Upper: at the empty graph, fixing the $m-1$ pairs at one vertex to absent isolates it, and a scorpion has no isolated vertex (the body dominates everything but the sting, and the sting has degree $1$). Lower: if a set $S$ of $|S| = k$ pairs leaves three vertices untouched, take them as $(s,t,b)$ -- every pair the triple touches is then free, and the feet may do anything -- so the subcube contains a scorpion and $S$ is not a $0$-certificate; hence $m - 2k \le 2$, i.e. every $0$-input costs at least $(m-2)/2$. Every $1$-input costs $\mathrm{C}^1 = 3m-6$. Exhaustive search gives $\mathrm{C}_{\min} = m-1$ exactly for $4 \le m \le 6$, attained only at graphs with an isolated vertex.
Graph connectivity total \(m-1\) putative Both kinds of certificate need $m-1$ coordinates and both achieve it: a spanning tree on the connected side, an isolated vertex on the disconnected side. So $\mathrm{C}_{\min} = \Theta(\sqrt{\mathrm{C}})$ -- connectivity is one of the cleanest natural examples of certificate complexity that varies quadratically across inputs.

  • Relations yes
    Inherited from \(\mathrm{C}(f)\), which is defined for relations: a certificate is a set of queries whose answers fix some valid output, and $\mathrm{C}_{\min}$ takes the min of $\mathrm{C}_x$ over $x$ rather than the max. Nothing in that reading needs a Boolean output. This flag previously read no, which silently dropped the recorded $\mathrm{C}_{\min}$ rows on \(\mathrm{SEARCH}_n\), \(\mathrm{Find1}_{\ge n/2}\), \(\mathrm{ApproxHW}\), \(S_{\mathrm{YZ}}\) and \(\mathrm{Ts}(G)\). Unlike $\mathrm{C}_{\min}$, the unambiguous variants \(\mathrm{UC}(f)\), \(\mathrm{UC}_{\min}(f)\) and \(\mathrm{SP}(f)\) stay undefined here, because unambiguity is a statement about the fibres of the output and a relation need not have any.
