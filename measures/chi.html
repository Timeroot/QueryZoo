\(\chi(f)\)

Conflict complexity

A measure of how hard it is for a decision tree to distinguish a random 0-input from a random 1-input, one query at a time.

Definition

Introduced to prove composition theorems. Informally: sample $x \sim \mu_0$ and $y \sim \mu_1$ from hard distributions on $g^{-1}(0)$ and $g^{-1}(1)$, and run a deterministic tree simultaneously on both, "resolving" a conflict when the tree queries a coordinate on which $x$ and $y$ differ. $\chi(g)$ measures the expected number of queries before a conflict, maximised over distributions and minimised over trees.

Basic facts

  • $\mathrm{R}(f\circ g^n) = \Omega(\mathrm{R}(f)\,\chi(g))$ [Sanyal18; AGJKLMSS17].
  • $\chi(g) = \Omega(\mathrm{bs}(g))$ -- more precisely $\chi(f)\ge(\mathrm{bs}(f)+1)/2$ for total $f$ [Li18, Thm 1].
  • $\chi(g) = \Omega(\sqrt{\mathrm{R}(g)})$.
  • $\chi\le\mathrm{D}$ gives $\chi\le\mathrm{C}^2$ and $\chi\le\mathrm{bs}^3$. The standard chain $\chi\le\bar\chi\le O(\mathrm{LR})\le O(\mathrm{R})$ also upper-bounds conflict complexity.
  • Both comparisons in Is … for total functions? are refuted. Ambainis–Iraids–Kokainis unambiguous DNF has $\mathrm{C}=\Theta(\chi^2)$, derived from [AIK26] and the standard inequalities; \(\mathrm{AND}_n \circ \mathrm{OR}_n\) has $\chi=\Omega(\mathrm{C}^2/\log^2\mathrm{C})$ [Meiburg26composition].
  • Conflict and sabotage are incomparable on total functions. \(P_q\) gives $\chi/\mathrm{RS}=\Omega(\log N)$; \(\mathrm{AND}_n \circ \mathrm{OR}_n\) gives $\mathrm{RS}/\chi=\Omega(\sqrt{\log N})$. Also $\chi$ is not $O(\mathrm{C})$ and fails perfect composition [Meiburg26composition].
