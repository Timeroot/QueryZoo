\(\mathrm{Adv}(f)\)

Positive-weights adversary bound

The original quantum adversary lower bound; all positive-weight variants coincide.

also written: Ambainis adversary, spectral adversary SA(f), unweighted/weighted adversary

Definition

$$\mathrm{Adv}(f) \;=\; \max_{\Gamma \ge 0,\ \Gamma \ne 0} \frac{\|\Gamma\|}{\max_i \|\Gamma \circ D_i\|},$$ the maximum over entrywise-nonnegative adversary matrices $\Gamma$ supported on pairs $(x,y)$ with $f(x)\ne f(y)$, where $D_i$ is the 0/1 matrix indicating $x_i \ne y_i$. Špalek-Szegedy showed the spectral, weighted, Kolmogorov and minimax adversary variants are all equal.

Basic facts

  • $\mathrm{Adv}(f) \le \mathrm{Adv}^{\pm}(f) = \Theta(\mathrm{Q}(f))$.
  • Certificate complexity barrier: $\mathrm{Adv}(f) \le \sqrt{\mathrm{C}^0(f)\,\mathrm{C}^1(f)}$ [SpalekSzegedy06, Zhang05]. Note it is the product that is capped: element distinctness has $\mathrm{C}^1 = 2$ but $\mathrm{C}^0 = n$, so the barrier only says $\mathrm{Adv} = O(\sqrt n)$. It is the property-testing promise problems, with both certificate complexities $O(1)$, on which the positive adversary collapses to $\mathrm{Adv} = O(1)$ — \(\mathrm{COL}_n\), \(\mathrm{SIMON}_n\) [HoyerSpalek05].
  • Sharper: $\mathrm{Adv}(f) \le \sqrt{\mathrm{RC}^0(f)\,\mathrm{RC}^1(f)}$, the randomized-certificate version of the barrier [KulkarniTal16].
  • $\lambda(f) \le \mathrm{Adv}(f)$ [ABKRT21 via BSS03].
  • For total search problems $\mathrm{Adv}$ can be exponentially smaller than $\widetilde{\deg}$ [BenDavidKundu24search].

The positive adversary is not obstructed here the way it is at \(\mathrm{COL}_n\) -- the certificate barrier $\mathrm{Adv} \le \sqrt{\mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1} = \Theta(n)$ is not binding -- but no matching lower bound is recorded.

Collision problem partial \(O(1)\) The certificate complexity barrier at its sharpest: a collision pair certifies a $2$-to-$1$ input with $2$ queries, and the promise means $O(1)$ queries also certify $1$-to-$1$-ness against the promise, so $\mathrm{Adv} \le \sqrt{\mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1} = O(1)$ while $\mathrm{Q} = \Theta(n^{1/3})$. No positive-weight adversary matrix can prove anything here -- this promise problem is the reason the negative-weights version had to be invented. HoyerSpalek05 SpalekSzegedy06
Element distinctness total \(O(\sqrt n)\) A colliding pair is a $2$-query certificate, but distinctness itself needs all $n$ values: with the convention $\mathrm{ED}_n = 1$ on all-distinct inputs, $\mathrm{C}^1 = n$ and $\mathrm{C}^0 = 2$. The barrier caps the product, so $\mathrm{Adv} \le \sqrt{2n} = O(\sqrt n)$ -- short of $\mathrm{Q} = \Theta(n^{2/3})$, but not the $O(1)$ that a one-sided reading of the barrier would suggest. Ambainis-Belovs function partial \(O(1)\)· putative The positive-weight adversary is at most quadratic in the exact degree even for partial functions (Anshu, Ben-David and Kundu), so $\mathrm{Adv} = O(\deg^2) = O(1)$ while $\mathrm{Q} = \Omega(n^{1/3})$. This is exactly why the paper cannot use the positive adversary, and it makes the function a second \(\mathrm{Adv}(f)\)-vs-\(\mathrm{Q}_{\varepsilon}(f)\) witness alongside \(\mathrm{SIMON}_n\) and \(\mathrm{COL}_n\).
Yamakawa-Zhandry problem relation \(\mathrm{poly}(n)\) A lower bound on $\mathrm{Q} = \mathrm{poly}(n)$.
Surjectivity total \(O(n/\sqrt{\log n})\) putative The certificate-complexity barrier: $\mathrm{Adv}(f) \le \sqrt{\mathrm{C}^0(f)\,\mathrm{C}^1(f)}$ for total $f$, and here that is $\sqrt{N \cdot n/2} = \Theta(n/\sqrt{\log n})$. Only a $\sqrt{\log n}$ factor short of $\mathrm{Q}$, so unlike \(\mathrm{ED}_n\) and \(\mathrm{COL}_n\) the positive-weights adversary is not badly broken on surjectivity -- but it cannot reach $\mathrm{Q} = \tilde\Theta(n)$ either. A floor: $\mathrm{Adv} \ge \lambda \ge \sqrt{\mathrm{s}} = \Omega(\sqrt n)$.
$k$-distinctness total \(O(\sqrt n)\) putative The certificate barrier $\mathrm{Adv}(f) \le \sqrt{\mathrm{C}^0 \mathrm{C}^1} = \sqrt{nk} = O(\sqrt n)$ for constant $k$ -- so the positive-weights adversary cannot even reach the $\Omega(n^{2/3})$ of \(\mathrm{ED}_n\), let alone the true exponent. This is why every lower bound in the row above goes through the polynomial method or a compressed oracle.
Triangle finding total \(\Theta(m)\) putative Pinned exactly, and it is the barrier in the $\mathrm{C}^1$ note made into a value. Upper: $\mathrm{Adv}(f) \le \sqrt{\mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1} = \sqrt{3\binom m2} = O(m)$. Lower: restrict to $K_{\lceil m/2\rceil,\lfloor m/2\rfloor}$ with the $2\binom{\lfloor m/2\rfloor}{2} = \Theta(m^2)$ within-side pairs left free; adding any one of them closes a triangle, so the restriction is $\mathrm{OR}$ on $\Theta(m^2)$ variables, and $\mathrm{Adv}$ is monotone under restriction, giving $\mathrm{Adv} \ge \mathrm{Adv}(\mathrm{OR}_{\Theta(m^2)}) = \Omega(m)$. So the positive-weights adversary is stuck at the trivial bound for a structural reason, and any improvement on $\Omega(m)$ needs negative weights or a different method entirely.
Recursive Fourier sampling partial \(\Theta(2^{\ell}) \text{ for } g = g_{\mathrm{mod}3}\) Unusually, the positive-weight adversary is already tight. Contrast \(\mathrm{TRI}_m\), where the certificate-complexity barrier pins $\mathrm{Adv}$ strictly below $\mathrm{Q}$. Note the $\mathrm{bs} = 1$ entry: the polynomial method cannot even get started, while the adversary method is exactly optimal. Aaronson03rfs
Welded trees relation \(O(n)\) $\mathrm{Adv}\le\mathrm{Adv}^{\pm}=\Theta(n)$ (\(\mathrm{Adv}(f)\) vs \(\mathrm{Adv}^{\pm}(f)\)). The positive-weight bound cannot be much smaller than $\mathrm{Q}=\Theta(n)$ here either, but no matching lower bound is recorded: the certificate structure is trivial ($\mathrm{C}=\Theta(1)$), which is exactly the regime where the positive adversary is weak.

Fourier fishing relation \(\Theta(1)\) At most $\mathrm{Adv}^{\pm} = \Theta(1)$ (\(\mathrm{Adv}(f)\) vs \(\mathrm{Adv}^{\pm}(f)\)), and at least $1$ since the problem is not trivial. The positive-weights bound is useless here for a reason that has nothing to do with the certificate barrier: $\mathrm{Q}$ itself is $1$.
Ordered search partial \(\Theta(1)\) putative Decision version, like the rest of the combinatorial column here. The certificate barrier applies: two queries $\{x_k = 0,\ x_{k+1} = 1\}$ pin the boundary, so $\mathrm{C}^0 = \mathrm{C}^1 = 2$ and $\mathrm{Adv} \le \sqrt{\mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1} = 2$. Against $\mathrm{Adv}^{\pm} = \widetilde\Theta(\log n)$ this is one of the cleanest positive-vs-negative-weight gaps in the zoo, and it is not the usual property-testing shape -- the promise is as far from a testing promise as it gets. For the $(n+1)$-valued reconstruction version the picture inverts: [ChildsLee08] compute the exact optimum of the adversary bound as $\tfrac1\pi\ln n + O(1)$, matching Hoyer-Neerbek-Shi, so the method is optimal there.
