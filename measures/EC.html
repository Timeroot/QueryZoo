\(\mathrm{EC}(f)\)

Expectational certificate complexity

A relaxation of certificate complexity in which the certificate is a distribution and only expected agreement is required.

Definition

$\mathrm{EC}(f)$ is the smallest $k$ for which there are weight vectors $w^x \in [0,1]^n$ with $\sum_i w^x_i \le k$ for every $x$, and $\sum_{i : x_i \ne y_i} w^x_i w^y_i \ge 1$ for all $x,y$ with $f(x)\ne f(y)$. It sits between fractional block sensitivity and certificate complexity and upper-bounds the classical adversary bound.

Basic facts

  • $\mathrm{fbs}(f) \le \mathrm{EC}(f) \le \mathrm{C}(f)$.
  • $\mathrm{CG}^{\mathrm{pub}}(f) = O(\mathrm{EC}(f))$ and $\mathrm{CG}(f) = O(\mathrm{EC}(f)^2)$ [CGLMSG23].
  • $\mathrm{EC}(f) \le \mathrm{R}_0(f) = O(\mathrm{EC}(f)^2)$ -- the "quadratically tight" relation that motivated the definition [GJKKLSSV17].

FORRELATION partial \(O(n^{3/4})\) putative Exhibiting a weighting directly, as one must off the promise ($\mathrm{EC} \le \mathrm{C}$ needs totality). Flipping one bit of $x$ or $y$ moves $\Phi$ by at most $2/\sqrt n$, so two promise inputs of opposite value differ in at least $(3/5-1/100)\sqrt n/2 \ge 0.29\sqrt n$ coordinates. Put $w^x_i = (0.29\sqrt n)^{-1/2}$ at every input and every coordinate: then $\sum_{i: x_i \ne y_i} w^x_i w^y_i \ge 1$, and the cost is $2n \cdot \Theta(n^{-1/4}) = \Theta(n^{3/4})$. This beats the trivial $\mathrm{EC} \le \mathrm{C} = \Theta(n)$; from below only $\mathrm{EC} \ge \mathrm{FC} = O(1)$ is available.
Simon's problem partial \(O(2^{n/2})\) putative Directly, not through $\mathrm{C}$: put $w^g_x = 2^{-(n-1)/2}$ at every one of the $2^n$ points. Two promise inputs of different value differ at $\ge 2^{n-1}$ points (turning a two-to-one $g$ into an injective one requires touching each of its $2^{n-1}$ colliding pairs), so $\sum_{x : g(x)\ne g'(x)} w^g_x w^{g'}_x \ge 1$, and each weight vector sums to $\sqrt 2\,2^{n/2}$. The textbook bound $\mathrm{EC} \le \mathrm{C}$ is a total-function statement and is not used.
Deutsch-Jozsa partial \(\Theta(\sqrt N)\) putative A genuinely intermediate value, strictly between $\mathrm{fbs} = 2$ and $\mathrm{C} = N/2+1$. Upper: put $w^x_i = 1/\sqrt N$ at the two constant inputs and $w^y_i = 2/\sqrt N$ everywhere at each balanced $y$; a constant $x$ and a balanced $y$ differ on $N/2$ coordinates, giving $\sum_{i : x_i \ne y_i} w^x_i w^y_i = (N/2)(2/N) = 1$, and every weight vector sums to at most $2\sqrt N$. Lower: at $x = 0^N$, for each balanced $y$ we need $1 \le \sum_{i \in \mathrm{supp}(y)} w^x_i w^y_i \le k \max_{i \in \mathrm{supp}(y)} w^x_i$, so fewer than $N/2$ coordinates can have $w^x_i < 1/k$; hence $k \ge \sum_i w^x_i \ge N/(2k)$.
Bernstein-Vazirani partial \(O(n)\) putative Weight $1$ on each of the $n$ positions $e_1,\dots,e_n$ and $0$ elsewhere, at every input. Two promise inputs with secrets $s \ne s'$ disagree at some $e_j$, since $s$ and $s'$ differ in some coordinate $j$, so the required product $\sum_{i : x_i \ne y_i} w^x_i w^y_i \ge 1$ holds and the scheme costs $n$. From below only $\mathrm{EC} \ge \mathrm{fbs} = \Theta(1)$; note $\mathrm{EC} \le \mathrm{C}$ is a total-function statement and is not what is used here.
Gap-Majority partial \(O(\sqrt{n/\delta})\) putative Uniform weights $w^x_i = (2\delta n)^{-1/2}$ at every input: two inputs of different value differ in $\ge 2\delta n$ coordinates, so their weighted product is $\ge 1$, at total cost $n/\sqrt{2\delta n} = \sqrt{n/(2\delta)}$. From below $\mathrm{EC} = \Omega(1/\delta)$: given weights of total mass $k$, take $x$ of weight $n/2+\delta n$ and let $\Delta$ be the $2\delta n$ lightest ones of $x$, whose total $w^x$-mass is at most $k\cdot 2\delta n/(n/2) = 4\delta k$; flipping them gives a $0$-input, and the constraint forces $4\delta k \ge 1$. So at constant $\delta$, $\mathrm{EC} = \Omega(1)$ and $O(\sqrt n)$ while $\mathrm{CA} = \Theta(1)$: the textbook chains $\mathrm{EC} \le \mathrm{C}$ and $\mathrm{R}_0 = O(\mathrm{EC}^2)$ are total-function statements and are not used.
Gap-Majority at constant gap partial \(O(\sqrt n)\) putative Uniform weights $w^x_i = (n/2)^{-1/2}$: two inputs of different value differ in $\ge n/2$ coordinates, so their weighted product is $\ge 1$, at total cost $\sqrt{2n}$. From below $\mathrm{EC} = \Omega(1)$ only, so this cell is not pinned -- but $O(\sqrt n)$ against $\mathrm{C} = \Theta(n)$ already refutes $\mathrm{EC} \le \mathrm{C}$-style tightness and, with $\mathrm{R}_0 = \Theta(n)$, refutes $\mathrm{R}_0 = O(\mathrm{EC}^2)$ on promises.
Approximate index partial \(O(k) = O(\log n)\) putative Weight $1$ on each of the $k$ address bits and on the two bits of the cell $a$ pointed at by the input's own address. Two inputs of different value with different addresses differ in some address bit, where both put weight $1$; with the same address they differ at cell $a$, which both weight. Cost $k + 2$. This does not go through $\mathrm{EC} \le \mathrm{C} = \Theta(1)$, which is a total-function statement -- its proof needs a $0$-certificate and a $1$-certificate to conflict, and here two non-conflicting certificates stitch together into a string outside the promise.
