\(\mathrm{cjdeg}_{\varepsilon}(f)\)

Conical junta degree

Least d such that f is approximated by a non-negative combination of width-d conjunctions (a conical junta).

also written: two-sided approximate non-negative degree, conical junta / non-negative-combination degree

Definition

A conical junta of width $d$ is a non-negative linear combination $h = \sum_C \alpha_C \, C$ of conjunctions $C$ of at most $d$ literals, with all $\alpha_C \ge 0$. The two-sided approximate non-negative degree $\mathrm{cjdeg}_\varepsilon$ is the least $d$ for which some width-$d$ conical junta $\varepsilon$-approximates $f$ pointwise. Non-negativity makes this a larger measure than approximate degree; it also upper-bounds randomized query complexity's LP relaxation and, in the usual regimes, exceeds $\mathrm{Q}$.

Parameters

\(\varepsilon\) (eps) default \(1/3\)

Basic facts

  • $\widetilde{\deg}_\varepsilon(f) \le \mathrm{cjdeg}_\varepsilon(f)$ (drop non-negativity).
  • Usually $\mathrm{cjdeg}(f) \ge \mathrm{Q}(f)$, so a separation of $\mathrm{Q}$ from $\mathrm{cjdeg}$ is strictly stronger than one from $\widetilde{\deg}$.
  • Total search problems: $\mathrm{Q}(S)$ can be exponentially larger than $\mathrm{cjdeg}(S)$ [BenDavidKundu24search]; previously not even a separation from $\mathrm{R}(S)$ was known.
  • The dual object is a conical junta certificate, which is the LP-dual view of randomized query lower bounds (the "partition bound" family).

OR total \(\Theta(n)\)· putative Upper: $\mathrm{cjdeg}_{\varepsilon}(f) \le \mathrm{D}(f) = n$, the $1$-leaves of the caterpillar being disjoint conjunctions. Lower: let $h = \sum_C \alpha_C C$ be a width-$d$ conical junta with $|h - \vee_n| \le 1/3$ pointwise. A conjunction with $C(e_i) = 1$ either has all its literals negative -- and then $C(0^n) = 1$ as well -- or has exactly one positive literal, necessarily $x_i$. Conjunctions of the first kind contribute at most $h(0^n) \le 1/3$ to each of the $n$ points $e_i$, so writing $A$ for the total weight of the conjunctions with exactly one positive literal, $2n/3 \le \sum_i h(e_i) \le n/3 + A$, i.e. $A \ge n/3$. Now evaluate under the product measure that sets each bit to $1$ with probability $1/d$: each such $C$ has expectation at least $\tfrac1d(1-\tfrac1d)^{d-1} \ge \tfrac{1}{ed}$, while $\mathbb{E}[h] \le 4/3$, so $A \le 4ed/3$ and $d \ge n/(4e)$. Non-negativity therefore costs a quadratic blow-up over $\widetilde{\deg} = \Theta(\sqrt n)$.
AND total \(\Theta(n)\)· putative Upper: $\mathrm{cjdeg}_{\varepsilon}(f) \le \mathrm{D}(f) = n$. Lower: let $h = \sum_C \alpha_C C$ be a width-$d$ conical junta with $|h - \wedge_n| \le 1/3$ pointwise. The conjunctions that evaluate to $1$ at $1^n$ are exactly the all-positive ones, so $\sum_{C \text{ positive}} \alpha_C = h(1^n) \ge 2/3$; an all-positive $C$ still evaluates to $1$ at $1^n - e_i$ unless $x_i$ is one of its at most $d$ literals, whence $\sum_i h(1^n - e_i) \ge \sum_{C \text{ positive}} \alpha_C\,(n - |C|) \ge (n-d)\tfrac23$. Every $1^n - e_i$ is a $0$-input, so the left-hand side is at most $n/3$, giving $d \ge n/2$. Non-negativity thus wipes out the $\widetilde{\deg} = \Theta(\sqrt n)$ saving, and it does so even though $\mathrm{sdeg}(\wedge_n) = 1$.

Element distinctness total \(\Theta(n)\) putative Upper: a randomized algorithm of cost $\mathrm{R}$ is a distribution over leaves, i.e. a width-$\mathrm{R}$ conical junta, so $\mathrm{cjdeg} \le \mathrm{R} = O(n)$. Lower: restrict to $x_i \in \{0,i\}$, which sends every block literal to a $z$-literal or a constant and hence maps conical juntas to conical juntas of no greater width. Let $h = \sum_C \alpha_C C$ have width $d$ and approximate "at most one $z_i = 1$". Only all-negative conjunctions fire at $z = 0$, so those carry total weight $\ge 2/3$; each such $C$, of support $S$, also fires at a weight-$2$ input $e_i + e_j$ unless $S$ meets $\{i,j\}$, which for a uniformly random pair happens with probability $\le 2d/n$. Averaging, $1/3 \ge (2/3)(1 - 2d/n)$, so $d \ge n/4$. This separates $\mathrm{cjdeg} = \Theta(n)$ from $\widetilde{\deg} = \Theta(n^{2/3})$: non-negativity costs the whole gap.

This is the strong form of the result: $\mathrm{cjdeg}$ is normally at least $\mathrm{R}$, and even an $\mathrm{R}$-vs-$\mathrm{cjdeg}$ separation for a total search problem was open.
