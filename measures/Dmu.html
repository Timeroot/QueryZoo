\(\mathrm{D}^{\mu}_{\varepsilon}(f)\)

Distributional query complexity

Least depth of a deterministic tree that is correct except with probability eps under the input distribution mu.

Definition

Fix a distribution $\mu$ on the domain. $\mathrm{D}^\mu_\varepsilon(f)$ is the smallest depth of a deterministic decision tree $T$ with $\Pr_{x\sim\mu}[T(x)\ne f(x)] \le \varepsilon$. Yao's minimax principle: $\mathrm{R}_\varepsilon(f) = \max_\mu \mathrm{D}^\mu_\varepsilon(f)$ (for the worst-case-cost version; see Yao's minimax and its failures for the subtleties with the expected-cost version $\bar{\mathrm{R}}_\varepsilon$).

Parameters

\(\varepsilon\) (eps) default \(1/3\)

Detailed records: param-minimax · \(\text{Yao minimax: } \bar{\mathrm{R}}_\varepsilon(f) = \Theta\big(\max_\mu \mathrm{D}^{\mu}_{\varepsilon}(f)\big)\)

\(\mu\) (mu) default \(worst case\)

input distribution

OR total \(\Theta(n)\) By Yao's minimax principle $\max_\mu \mathrm{D}^{\mu}_{1/3}(f) = \mathrm{R}_{1/3}(f) = \Theta(n)$. A concrete maximiser: half the mass on $0^n$, the other half spread uniformly over the $n$ weight-one inputs. A depth-$d$ tree that has seen only zeros is consistent with $0^n$ and with the $n-d$ unqueried $e_i$, so whichever answer it commits to it errs on mass at least $\min\{1/2,\ (n-d)/(2n)\}$, forcing $d \ge n/3$. See \(\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{prod}}(f)\) for the product-distribution version. Yao77
AND total \(\min\{n,\ \lceil \log(1/\varepsilon)/\log(1/p)\rceil\} \text{ under } \mu_p\) The cleanest illustration of why $\mathrm{D}^{\mu}$ is a family of measures rather than one number. Let $\mu_p$ make each bit $1$ independently with probability $p$. Querying bits until one comes up $0$ and answering $1$ if the first $m$ are all $1$ errs with probability $p^{m}(1-p^{n-m})$, so $m = \lceil \log(1/\varepsilon)/\log(1/p)\rceil$ queries suffice; conversely, the leaf reached by the all-ones input has some depth $m_0 \le m$ and carries $\mathrm{AND}^{-1}(0)$-mass $p^{m_0} - p^{n}$ against $\mathrm{AND}^{-1}(1)$-mass $p^{n}$, so the error is at least $\min\{p^{m}-p^{n},\,p^{n}\}$. At $p = 1/2$ this is $\Theta(\log(1/\varepsilon))$; at $p = 1 - 1/n$ it is $n$. Averaging over the parameter is exactly what the zoo cannot do, which is why $\mathrm{D}^{\mu}$ is left out of the ladder ordering on the matrix page.
PARITY total \(n\) Under the uniform $\mu$, for every $\varepsilon < 1/2$ -- and since $\mathrm{D}^\mu \le \mathrm{D} = n$ for every $\mu$, the uniform distribution is simultaneously the hardest one, so no choice of $\mu$ has to be recorded alongside the value. Every leaf of a tree of depth $< n$ leaves some coordinate unqueried, and conditioned on the queried bits the parity is still uniform on $\{0,1\}$; so any such tree errs with probability exactly $1/2$ under the uniform distribution. This is the extreme point of Yao's principle: one distribution already witnesses the worst case, with no slack.
