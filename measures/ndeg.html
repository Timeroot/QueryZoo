Measures › Polynomial degree
\(\mathrm{ndeg}(f)\)

Nondeterministic degree

Least degree of a polynomial that is nonzero exactly on the 1-inputs.

Definition

The least degree of $p$ with $p(x)\ne 0 \iff f(x)=1$ (on the domain). de Wolf: $\mathrm{NQ}(f)=\lceil\mathrm{ndeg}(f)/2\rceil$, and $\mathrm{rdeg}(f)=\max\{\mathrm{ndeg}(f),\mathrm{ndeg}(\bar f)\}$.

Basic facts

  • $\mathrm{ndeg}(f)\le\mathrm{C}^1(f)$.

Symmetric functions (general) total \(\Theta(\#\{j \,:\, f_j = 0\})\) putative Upper: $\prod_{j : f_j = 0}(|x|-j)$ is nonzero exactly on the $1$-inputs. EXACT-HALF total \(n/2\) putative Exactly $n/2$, for $\mathrm{EH}_n$ itself. Upper bound: for a perfect matching $M$ of $[n]$ put $q_M(x)=\prod_{(i,j)\in M}(x_i-x_j)$, of degree $n/2$; it is nonzero iff every pair of $M$ is split, which forces $|x| = n/2$, and every middle input is split by some matching. So a generic combination $\sum_M a_M q_M$ is nonzero exactly on the middle slice. Lower bound: if $p$ is nonzero exactly on $\mathrm{EH}^{-1}(1)$ then $p^2$ is nonnegative with the same zero set, so its symmetrisation $P(k)=\mathbb{E}_{|x|=k}[p(x)^2]$ is a univariate polynomial of degree $\le 2\deg p$ vanishing at all $n$ points $k \ne n/2$ of $\{0,\dots,n\}$; hence $\deg p \ge n/2$. Contrast $\mathrm{ndeg}(\overline{\mathrm{EH}}_n) = 1$, witnessed by $\sum_i x_i - n/2$: this factor-$n/2$ asymmetry between a function and its negation is what \(\mathrm{EH}_n\) is for, and it is what makes $\mathrm{rdeg} = \max\{\mathrm{ndeg}(f),\mathrm{ndeg}(\bar f)\} = n/2$ here. Deutsch-Jozsa partial \(1\) putative Nondeterministic degree collapses where ordinary degree does not. Take $p = \sum_i c_i x_i$ with $\sum_i c_i = 0$ and the $c_i$ otherwise generic: then $p$ vanishes at $0^N$ and at $1^N$, while $\sum_{i \in \mathrm{supp}(y)} c_i \ne 0$ for every balanced $y$ because generic points of the hyperplane $\sum c_i = 0$ avoid the finitely many further hyperplanes. So $\mathrm{ndeg} = 1 < \deg = 2$.

Gap-Majority partial \(\Theta(n)\) putative Same symmetrisation, applied to $p^2$: if $p$ is nonzero exactly on the $1$-inputs then $q(t) = \mathbb{E}_{|x|=t}[p(x)^2]$ is a non-negative univariate polynomial of degree $\le 2\,\mathrm{ndeg}$ vanishing at all $(1/2-\delta)n$ integers $t \le n/2-\delta n$ and nonzero above $n/2+\delta n$, so $\mathrm{ndeg} \ge (1/4-\delta/2)n$; and $\mathrm{ndeg} \le \mathrm{C}^1 = \Theta(n)$.
Gap-Majority at constant gap partial \(\Theta(n)\) putative Same symmetrisation applied to $p^2$; and $\mathrm{ndeg} \le \mathrm{C}^1$.
Parity-labelled FIND-1 partial \(1\) putative $\sum_{i \text{ odd}} x_i$ is nonzero on the domain exactly at the $1$-inputs.
Approximate index partial \(\Theta(1)\) $\mathrm{ndeg} \le \mathrm{C}^1 = \Theta(1)$ -- take the sum over $1$-certificates of the product of their literal indicators, which is nonzero exactly on the $1$-inputs of the domain (a $0$-input containing a $1$-certificate would be a domain point of both values). And $\mathrm{ndeg} \ge 1$.
Collision problem partial \(2\) putative Exactly $2$: the polynomial $\sum_{i<j}\sum_v x_{i,v}x_{j,v}$ counts colliding pairs, so it is nonzero precisely on the two-to-one side, and no linear form can do that. Equivalently $\mathrm{ndeg} \le \mathrm{C}^1 = 2$.
Element distinctness total \(\Theta(n)\) putative Upper: $\mathrm{ndeg} \le \mathrm{C}^1 = n$. Lower: restrict to $x_i \in \{0,i\}$ as in the \(\deg(f)\) entry, so that $\mathrm{ED}_n$ becomes "at most one $z_i = 1$". A multilinear $p$ that is nonzero exactly on the $n+1$ points of weight $\le 1$ has Moebius coefficients $c_S = (-1)^{|S|}(a_0 - \sum_{i \in S} a_i)$ for $|S| \ge 1$, where $a_0 = p(0)$ and $a_i = p(e_i)$ are all nonzero. Killing every coefficient of degree $n$ forces $a_0 = \sum_i a_i$, and killing every coefficient of degree $n-1$ forces $a_0 = \sum_{i \ne j} a_i$ for each $j$; together these give $a_j = 0$, a contradiction. Hence $\mathrm{ndeg} \ge n-1$.
Ambainis-Belovs function partial \(O(1)\) The exact representation $T(K(x)/n)$ is nonzero exactly on the $1$-inputs of the domain, so $\mathrm{ndeg} \le \deg \le 9$.
Triangle finding total \(3\) putative Upper: $\sum_{\{a,b,c\}}x_{ab}x_{bc}x_{ca}$ is a sum of non-negative terms, positive exactly on the graphs with a triangle, so $\mathrm{ndeg}\le\mathrm{C}^1=3$. Lower: fix one triple free and every other edge to $0$; a multilinear polynomial in three variables vanishing on the other seven points must be a multiple of $x_1x_2x_3$, and nondeterministic degree does not increase under restriction. deWolf03
Ordered search partial \(1\) putative A linear form suffices: pick $c_i$ so that $\sum_{i>k}c_i$ is $0$ for even $k$ and $1$ for odd $k$ (successive differences are $\pm1$), giving $p$ nonzero exactly on the $1$-inputs of the promise. Nondeterministic degree is defined on the domain only, so nothing off the chain constrains $p$; and $\mathrm{ndeg}\ge1$ since the function is nonconstant.
Inner product mod 2 total \(\Theta(n)\) putative Upper: $p = 1 - \prod_{i}(1-2x_iy_i) = 1-(-1)^{x\cdot y}$ is nonzero exactly on $\mathrm{IP}^{-1}(1)$, degree $n$. Lower: any nondeterministic polynomial $p$ gives a sign-representing one -- $q(x) := \delta - p(x)^2$ is $>0$ on $f^{-1}(0)$ and $<0$ on $f^{-1}(1)$ for any $\delta$ below the least positive value of $p^2$ -- so $\mathrm{sdeg}(f) \le 2\,\mathrm{ndeg}(f)$ always, and $\mathrm{sdeg} = \Theta(n)$ here. Bentness leaves no room for a nondeterministic shortcut.
Minsky-Papert one-in-a-box total \(m = N^{1/3}\) putative Upper: $\mathrm{ndeg} \le \mathrm{C}^1 = m$. Lower: planting a $0$ in all but one bit of each box restricts MP to $\mathrm{AND}_m$, and a polynomial nonzero exactly at $1^m$ must be a multiple of $\prod_i x_i$, so $\mathrm{ndeg}(\mathrm{AND}_m) = m$; restrictions do not increase $\mathrm{ndeg}$.
Sink of a tournament total \(m-1 = \Theta(\sqrt N)\) putative Upper: $\mathrm{ndeg} \le \mathrm{C}^1 = m-1$. Lower: fix a sink-free tournament on $V \setminus \{v\}$ and leave the $m-1$ edges at $v$ free; the restriction is $\mathrm{AND}_{m-1}$, whose nondeterministic degree is $m-1$ (a polynomial nonzero exactly at $1^k$ is a multiple of $\prod_i x_i$), and restrictions do not increase $\mathrm{ndeg}$.
Scorpion graphs total \(\Theta(m)\) putative Upper: for $m \ge 5$ the property is a disjoint union of $m(m-1)(m-2)$ subcubes of codimension $3m-6$, and the sum of their indicator monomial products is nonzero exactly on the scorpions, so $\mathrm{ndeg} \le 3m-6$. Lower: the restriction of the \(\mathrm{Q}_{0}(f)\) entry turns $\mathrm{SCORP}_m$ into $\mathrm{AND}_{m-3}$, a polynomial nonzero exactly at $1^k$ is a multiple of $\prod_i x_i$, and restrictions never increase $\mathrm{ndeg}$, so $\mathrm{ndeg} \ge m-3$.
Graph connectivity total \(m-1\) putative Exactly. Upper: the spanning-tree generating polynomial $\tau(G) = \sum_{T}\prod_{e \in T}x_e$ (the reduced Laplacian determinant of the matrix-tree theorem) is multilinear of degree $m-1$ and is nonzero precisely when $G$ is connected, all its terms being non-negative. Lower: fix a spanning tree's $m-1$ pairs free and all other pairs absent; the restriction is $\mathrm{AND}_{m-1}$, a polynomial nonzero exactly at $1^{k}$ is a multiple of $\prod_i x_i$, and restrictions never increase $\mathrm{ndeg}$. So the nondeterministic degree of connectivity is $\Theta(\sqrt N)$, a quadratic drop from $\deg = N$.
