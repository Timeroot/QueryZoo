\(\mathrm{R}_{\varepsilon}(f)\)

Bounded-error randomized query complexity

Worst-case number of queries of a randomized decision tree that errs with probability at most eps.

also written: R_2(f), Monte Carlo decision tree complexity, BPP-style query complexity

Definition

A randomized decision tree is a probability distribution over deterministic trees. $\mathrm{R}_\varepsilon(f)$ is the least $T$ such that some distribution over depth-$T$ trees outputs $f(x)$ with probability $\ge 1-\varepsilon$ for every $x$ in the domain. Unadorned $\mathrm{R}(f) := \mathrm{R}_{1/3}(f)$.

Two cost conventions circulate and they are not interchangeable at small bias:

  • worst-case cost ($\mathrm{R}_\varepsilon$): every branch has depth $\le T$;
  • expected cost ($\bar{\mathrm{R}}_\varepsilon$, see \(\bar{\mathrm{R}}_{\varepsilon}(f)\)): the expected number of queries is $\le T$, maximised over inputs.

Parameters

\(\varepsilon\) (eps) default \(1/3\)range \([0, 1/2)\)

Error probability. Standard choice 1/3; "small bias" means eps = 1/2 - gamma with gamma -> 0.

Amplification (always true): $\mathrm{R}_{\varepsilon}(f) = O\!\big(\mathrm{R}_{1/3}(f)\log(1/\varepsilon)\big)$ by majority vote, and this is tight for some $f$ (e.g. $\mathrm{OR}$) but not for all.

Small bias: for $\varepsilon = 1/2-\gamma$, $\mathrm{R}_{1/2-\gamma}(f) \ge \Omega(\gamma^2 \mathrm{R}_{1/3}(f))$ is false in general; the correct general statement is $\mathrm{R}_{1/3}(f) = O\!\big(\gamma^{-2}\,\mathrm{R}_{1/2-\gamma}(f)\big)$ by amplification, and the extent to which small-bias algorithms can beat $\gamma^2$ scaling is exactly what the measures \(\mathrm{noisyR}(f)\) and \(\mathrm{LR}(f)\) were invented to capture.

Detailed records: \(\mathrm{R}_{\varepsilon}(f) \text{ as } \varepsilon \to 0\)

Basic facts

  • $\mathrm{bs}(f) \le 3\,\mathrm{R}(f)$ and $\widetilde{\deg}(f) \le \mathrm{R}(f)$ [Nisan; BBCMdW01].
  • $\mathrm{D}(f) \le 27\,\mathrm{R}(f)^3$ [Nisan]. Best known separation is $\mathrm{D} = \tilde\Omega(\mathrm{R}^2)$ [ABBLSS17], so the truth is between $2$ and $3$.
  • Composition can fail: there are partial $f,g$ with $\mathrm{R}(f\circ g) \ll \mathrm{R}(f)\mathrm{R}(g)$ [BenDavidBlais20composition].
  • $\mathrm{R}_\varepsilon(f) = \max_\mu \mathrm{D}^\mu_\varepsilon(f)$ (Yao), for the worst-case-cost convention.
  • Composition holds with an outer-input-size logarithmic loss, even for partial Boolean functions: $\mathrm{R}(f\circ g^n)=\Omega(\mathrm{R}(f)\mathrm{R}(g)/\log(n+1))$ [PednekarPodder26STOC]. Constant-factor composition fails even for total functions [Meiburg26composition]; see comp-R.
  • The optimal total-function $\mathrm{R}$-versus-$\mathrm{Q}$ power is 4 [OpenAI26QuarticRQ]. The new family rules out every power below 4, matching $\mathrm{R}\le\mathrm{D}=O(\mathrm{Q}^4)$ [ABKRT21]; see \(F_{k,m}\).
