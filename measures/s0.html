\(\mathrm{s}^0(f)\)

0-sensitivity

Sensitivity maximised only over 0-inputs.

Definition

$\mathrm{s}^0(f) = \max_{x \in f^{-1}(0)} \mathrm{s}_x(f)$.

Basic facts

  • $\mathrm{s}(f) = \max(\mathrm{s}^0(f), \mathrm{s}^1(f))$.
  • $\lambda(f) \le \sqrt{\mathrm{s}^0(f)\,\mathrm{s}^1(f)}$.

Rubinstein's function total \(n = \sqrt N\) putative At a $0$-input the pivotal bits are those that make some block fire. Surjectivity total \(\Theta(n/\log n)\) putative A factor $\log n$ below $\mathrm{s}^1$. Upper: at a $0$-input, a single flip can only change one slot, so it can only add one value to the range; hence exactly one value $u$ may be missing and each slot has at most one sensitive bit, giving $\mathrm{s}^0 \le N = \Theta(n/\log n)$. Lower: take $u = 0^r$, cover the $R-1-r$ values of Hamming weight $\ge 2$ with one slot each and fill the remaining $N-R+1+r$ slots with duplicated weight-one values; flipping the single $1$ of any of those slots produces $u$ and loses nothing. Exhaustive check at $R=4$, $N=8$: $\mathrm{s}^0 = 7 = N-R+1+r$.
$k$-distinctness total \(\Theta(n)\) putative On the $0$-input of the $\mathrm{C}^0$ note, overwriting any of the $n-k+1$ positions outside the $(k-1)$-group with that group's symbol flips the answer. So the sensitivity is wildly asymmetric: $\Theta(n)$ on $0$-inputs against $k$ on $1$-inputs.
Triangle finding total \(\Theta(m^2) = \Theta(n)\) putative The maximum in the $\mathrm{s}$ entry is attained at the $0$-input $K_{\lceil m/2\rceil,\lfloor m/2\rfloor}$, so it is the $0$-side that carries it.
Recursive Fourier sampling partial \(0\) Immediate from $\mathrm{s} = \max(\mathrm{s}^0,\mathrm{s}^1) = 0$.
Ordered search partial \(2\) putative Attained at any interior $k$ of even parity, $n \ge 3$.
Inner product mod 2 total \(\Theta(n)\) putative From $\mathrm{s}(x,y) = |x|+|y|$: the maximum $n$ is only at $x=y=1^m$ (value $m \bmod 2$) and the other side tops out at $n-1$. So $\{\mathrm{s}^0,\mathrm{s}^1\} = \{n, n-1\}$.
Minsky-Papert one-in-a-box total \(m^2\) As above.
Sink of a tournament total \(3\) Stated for $m \ge 3$. At a $0$-input, flipping the edge $e$ creates a sink iff $e$ is the unique out-edge of some vertex. Distinct vertices have distinct unique out-edges, so $\mathrm{s}^0_x$ counts the vertices of out-degree exactly $1$; if there are $k$ of them, the sub-tournament they span has $\binom k2$ edges and contributes $\binom k2$ to their out-degrees, whence $\binom k2 \le k$ and $k \le 3$. Three is attained: orient $u_1 \to u_2 \to u_3 \to u_1$ and every other edge into $\{u_1,u_2,u_3\}$. So $\mathrm{s}^0 = 3$ -- a constant, against $\mathrm{bs}^0 \ge m$ (the out-edge sets of the $m$ vertices are disjoint blocks, and flipping one makes its vertex a sink). SINK is thus a clean natural witness to a quadratic-and-then-some $\mathrm{s}^0$-versus-$\mathrm{bs}^0$ gap.
Scorpion graphs total \(2m-4\) putative The extremal $0$-input is $K_{m-2} \sqcup K_2$: a clique on $A$, plus a lone edge $\{u,v\}$ with no edges to $A$. Adding $ua$ for any $a \in A$ makes a scorpion with sting $v$, tail $u$, body $a$ (the clique makes $a$ dominate everything but $v$); symmetrically for $va$. That is $2(m-2)$ sensitive pairs, and exhaustive search confirms $\mathrm{s}^0 = 2m-4$ exactly for $m \le 7$. Contrast $\mathrm{s}^1 = 3m-6$: the scorpion is mildly $1$-biased.
Graph connectivity total \(\lfloor m^2/4 \rfloor\) putative At a disconnected $G$ the sensitive coordinates are exactly the absent pairs whose insertion connects the whole graph, so $G$ must have precisely two components and every crossing pair is sensitive. Balancing them gives $\lfloor m/2\rfloor\lceil m/2\rceil$. Deleting an edge from a disconnected graph never connects it, so nothing else contributes.
