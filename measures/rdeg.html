\(\mathrm{rdeg}(f)\)

Rational degree

Least d such that f = p/q on the domain with deg(p), deg(q) <= d.

Definition

$\mathrm{rdeg}_\varepsilon(f)$ is the least $\max\{\deg p,\deg q\}$ over rational functions $p/q$ with $|p(x)/q(x) - f(x)|\le\varepsilon$ on $\mathrm{Dom}(f)$; $\mathrm{rdeg}:=\mathrm{rdeg}_0$. No boundedness is required off the domain, which is what makes it behave so differently from $\widetilde{\deg}$.

Characterisation: $\mathrm{rdeg}(f)=\max\{\mathrm{ndeg}(f),\mathrm{ndeg}(\bar f)\}$, hence $\mathrm{rdeg}(f)=\mathrm{rdeg}(\bar f)$ and $\mathrm{rdeg}(f)\le \mathrm{C}(f)$.

Parameters

\(\varepsilon\) (eps) default \(0\)

Basic facts

  • Symmetric $f$: $\mathrm{rdeg}(f)=\Omega(\deg(f))$ (tight).
  • Unate $f$: $\mathrm{rdeg}(f)=\Omega(\sqrt{\deg(f)})$ (tight).
  • Read-once $\mathsf{AC}^0$/$\mathsf{TC}^0$ formulas: $\mathrm{rdeg}(f)=\Omega(\sqrt{\deg(f)})$; with parity gates and depth $d$, $\Omega(\deg(f)^{1/2d})$.
  • Almost every $f$ on $n$ variables has $\mathrm{rdeg}(f)\ge n/2-O(\sqrt n)$.
  • $\deg_{\pm}(f)\le 2\,\mathrm{rdeg}(f)$, tight on parity; and $\mathrm{rdeg}(f)=\Omega(\log n)$ for any $f$ depending on all $n$ variables, tight on \(\mathrm{ADDR}_k\) [KKWY26].
  • Fortnow's question, answered in 2026: $\deg(f)=\widetilde O(\mathrm{rdeg}(f)^3)$ and $\mathrm{D}(f)\le16\,\mathrm{rdeg}(f)^4$ [KKWY26]. The rdeg column of the big matrix read "?" from 1994 until then; what is left is the exponent, see What is the exponent of … versus …?.

Equivalent / near-equivalent measures

  • \(\mathrm{PostQ}_{\varepsilon}(f)\) For matching error: $\mathrm{rdeg}_\varepsilon(f) = \Theta(\mathrm{PostQ}_\varepsilon(f))$. At $\varepsilon = 0$ this is $\mathrm{PostQ}_{\mathrm{E}}$, not the bounded-error \(\mathrm{PostQ}_{\varepsilon}(f)\); the bounded-error pairing is with \(\mathrm{rdeg}_{\varepsilon}(f)\). Minsky-Papert one-in-a-box total \(m^2 = N^{2/3}\) putative $\mathrm{rdeg}(f) = \max\{\mathrm{ndeg}(f), \mathrm{ndeg}(\bar f)\}$. The complement side dominates: filling every other box with a $1$ restricts $\overline{\mathrm{MP}}$ to $\mathrm{NOR}_{m^2}$, whose nondeterministic degree is $m^2$ (a polynomial nonzero exactly at $0^{k}$ is a multiple of $\prod_i(1-x_i)$), and $\mathrm{ndeg}(\bar f) \le \mathrm{C}^0 = m^2$. So $\mathrm{rdeg} = m^2 = \mathrm{C}$, a cube root below $\deg = N$.
Sink of a tournament total \(\Theta(m) = \Theta(\sqrt N)\) putative Sandwiched: $\mathrm{rdeg}(f) = \max\{\mathrm{ndeg}(f),\mathrm{ndeg}(\bar f)\} \ge m-1$, and $\mathrm{rdeg}(f) \le \mathrm{C}(f) = m$. (For the record the complement is no harder: at most one vertex is a sink, so $\overline{\mathrm{SINK}} = 1 - \sum_v \prod_{u\ne v}[u\to v]$ on the nose, giving $\mathrm{ndeg}(\bar f) \le m-1$ as well.)

Graph connectivity total \(\Theta(m^2) = \Theta(N)\) putative Rational functions buy essentially nothing. Use the characterisation $\mathrm{rdeg}(f) = \max\{\mathrm{ndeg}(f),\mathrm{ndeg}(\bar f)\} \le \mathrm{C}(f)$: the $0$-side is expensive, since fixing all pairs inside a balanced bipartition $(A,B)$ to present and leaving the $\lfloor m^2/4\rfloor$ crossing pairs free makes the restriction $\mathrm{OR}$, whence $\mathrm{ndeg}(\bar f) \ge \mathrm{ndeg}(\mathrm{NOR}_{\lfloor m^2/4\rfloor}) = \lfloor m^2/4\rfloor$; and $\mathrm{C} = \Theta(m^2)$ caps it. Contrast $\mathrm{ndeg} = m-1$: the asymmetry between spanning trees and cuts is exactly what rational degree cannot exploit.
