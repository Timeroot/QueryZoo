\(\mathrm{noisyR}(f)\)

Noisy randomized query complexity

Randomized complexity when the algorithm may buy a biased (noisy) copy of any bit at cost proportional to bias squared.

Definition

In the noisy query model the algorithm may, at any step, request the value of $x_i$ through a channel of its choosing with bias $\gamma$, paying cost $\Theta(\gamma^2)$. (Equivalently: it may make a fractional query.) $\mathrm{noisyR}(f)$ is the least total cost of an algorithm that computes $f$ with error $\le 1/3$.

Ben-David and Blais showed this is exactly the right outer measure for composition.

Basic facts

  • $\mathrm{noisyR}(f) \le \mathrm{R}(f)$ always, and the two are not within a constant: $\mathrm{R}$ can be exponentially larger for partial $f$ (\(\mathrm{ApproxIndex}_k\)). Whether $\mathrm{R} = 2^{O(\mathrm{noisyR})}$ in general is open.
  • $\mathrm{R}(f\circ g^n) = \Omega(\mathrm{noisyR}(f)\,\mathrm{R}(g))$, and $\mathrm{noisyR}$ is the largest measure with this property [BenDavidBlais20composition].
  • Characterisation: $\mathrm{noisyR}(f) = \Theta\!\big(\mathrm{R}(f\circ \mathrm{GapMaj}_n)/\mathrm{R}(\mathrm{GapMaj}_n)\big)$.
  • If $\mathrm{R}(f) = \Theta(n)$ then $\mathrm{noisyR}(f) = \Theta(\mathrm{R}(f))$.
  • Lemma 37 of [BenDavidBlais20composition]: $\mathrm{noisyR}(f) = \Omega(1)$ for every non-constant $f$, and more precisely distinguishing two inputs that differ on a block $B$ costs $\Omega(1)$ inside $B$. Both hold for relations with two inputs whose allowed output sets are disjoint.
  • Lemma 38: $\mathrm{noisyR}(f) = \Omega(\mathrm{fbs}(f))$, by LP-weighting the previous item over the sensitive blocks. Composed with $\mathrm{R} = O(\mathrm{fbs}^3)$ this gives a complexity-only bound for total $f$. The new input-size-dependent bound is $\mathrm{R}=O(\mathrm{noisyR}\log(n+1))$ for partial Boolean functions [PednekarPodder26STOC].
  • Normal form (Lemmas 36, Theorem 39): an optimal noisy algorithm may be assumed to use only two bias values, $\gamma = 1$ and $\gamma = 1/\sqrt n$.
  • For every nonconstant partial Boolean $f$ on $n$ bits, $\mathrm{R}(f)\le C\log(n+1)\mathrm{noisyR}(f)$ [PednekarPodder26STOC, Theorem 31]. This closes Is …?. Total functions nevertheless have $\mathrm{R}/\mathrm{noisyR}=\Omega((\log N)^{1/4}/\log\log\log N)$ [Meiburg26composition, Theorem 4.1].

Sink of a tournament total \(\Theta(m) = \Theta(\sqrt N)\) putative The zoo's shortcut $\mathrm{R} = \Theta(n) \Rightarrow \mathrm{noisyR} = \Theta(\mathrm{R})$ does not apply -- $\mathrm{R} = \Theta(\sqrt N)$ here, not linear in the arity -- so the lower bound has to come from a restriction. Fix the tournament on $\{2,\dots,m\}$ to be transitive and orient $\{1,m\}$ into vertex $1$: then every vertex except $1$ has an outgoing edge, and the function of the $m-2$ free edges $\{1,u\}$ is exactly $\mathrm{AND}_{m-2}$. Noisy randomized complexity is monotone under restriction, and $\mathrm{noisyR}(\mathrm{AND}_k) = \Theta(k)$ since $\mathrm{R}(\mathrm{AND}_k) = k$ is linear in its arity. Scorpion graphs total \(\Theta(m) = \Theta(\sqrt N)\) putative As on \(\mathrm{SINK}_m\), the "$\mathrm{R}$ linear in the arity" shortcut is unavailable ($\mathrm{R} = \Theta(\sqrt N)$), so the lower bound comes from a restriction to $\mathrm{AND}$. Put the sting at $1$, the tail at $2$ and the body at $3$: fix $\{1,2\}$ and $\{2,3\}$ present, every other edge at $1$ or $2$ absent, and every foot-foot edge absent. What remains free is the $m-3$ edges $\{3,u\}$, $u \ge 4$, and the graph is a scorpion iff all of them are present -- no other vertex can play body, since the feet now have degree $\le 1$. So $\mathrm{noisyR} \ge \mathrm{noisyR}(\mathrm{AND}_{m-3}) = \Theta(m)$, and $\mathrm{noisyR} \le \mathrm{R} = \Theta(m)$.
