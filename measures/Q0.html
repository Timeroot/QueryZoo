\(\mathrm{Q}_{0}(f)\)

Zero-error (Las Vegas) quantum query complexity

Quantum algorithm that never errs but may output "don't know" with probability <= 1/2.

Definition

The quantum analogue of $\mathrm{R}_0$: outputs in $\{0,1,\bot\}$, never wrong, $\Pr[\bot]\le 1/2$; cost is the worst-case number of queries. The $\bot$ outcome is what makes a worst-case count meaningful -- as with $\mathrm{R}_0$, an always-answering algorithm with a worst-case bound collapses to $\mathrm{D}$. Charging expected cost instead and never outputting $\bot$ gives the same measure up to a factor of two (Markov in one direction, repetition in the other), and the same conversion applied to a classical Las Vegas algorithm gives $\mathrm{Q}_0(f) \le 2\,\mathrm{R}_0(f)$.

Basic facts

  • $\mathrm{Q}(f) \le \mathrm{Q}_0(f) \le \mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{E}}(f)$, and $\mathrm{Q}_0(f) \le 2\,\mathrm{R}_0(f)$.
  • The polynomial floor under $\mathrm{Q}_0$ is $\mathrm{rdeg}/2$, not $\deg/2$. Writing $p_b$ for the probability of output $b$ after $T$ queries -- a polynomial of degree $\le 2T$ -- zero error says only that $p_1$ vanishes on $f^{-1}(0)$ and $p_0$ vanishes on $f^{-1}(1)$. So $p_1$ is a nondeterministic polynomial for $f$ and $p_0$ one for $\bar f$, giving $\mathrm{Q}_0(f) \ge \tfrac12\max\{\mathrm{ndeg}(f), \mathrm{ndeg}(\bar f)\} = \mathrm{rdeg}(f)/2 \ge \mathrm{NQ}(f) - \tfrac12$ -- a zero-error algorithm is in particular a nondeterministic one for $f$ and for $\bar f$. The stronger $\deg(f) \le 2T$ needs $p_1 = f$ exactly, which is $\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{E}}$; the difference is real, and it is large -- on \(\bar{\wedge}\text{-tree}\) $\deg = N$ while $\mathrm{rdeg} = \Theta(\sqrt N)$.
  • For total $f$, $\mathrm{Q}_0$ is polynomially related to all the other measures; the exact relations to $\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{E}}$ are less studied than the $\mathrm{R}_0$-vs-$\mathrm{R}$ ones.
  • New certification lower bounds: $\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{E}}^{\mathrm{cert}}=\Theta(\mathrm{Q}_0^{\mathrm{cert}})=O(\mathrm{Q}_0)$, $\overline{\deg}^{\mathrm{cert}}=O(\mathrm{Q}_0)$, and $\overline{\mathrm{osdeg}}=O(\mathrm{Q}_0)$ [KLLPV26].
  • AND of complemented EXACT-ONE functions has $\mathrm{Q}_0=\widetilde\Theta(m^{3/2})$ but $\mathrm{Q}=\Theta(m)$ and $\mathrm{rdeg}=m$; bounded degree certification proves the stronger lower bound [KLLPV26, Theorem 10 and Lemma 55].

FIND-1 under a density promise relation \(\Theta(1)\) The output is self-verifying -- the algorithm only ever names an index it has seen to be $1$ -- so the bounded-error algorithm is already zero-error: $\mathrm{Q}_0 \le \mathrm{R}_0 = O(1)$. Contrast \(\mathrm{SEARCH}_n\), where $\mathrm{Q}_0 = n$ because the all-zero input has no witness; the density promise removes that input.
Approximate Hamming weight relation \(\Theta(n)\)· putative Zero error forces a certificate on every branch, quantumly too: a zero-error algorithm's final state is supported on outcomes valid for every input consistent with what it has learned, and by $\mathrm{C}_{\min} = (1-2\varepsilon)n$ nothing short of that many queries pins an answer down. And $\mathrm{Q}_0 \le \mathrm{R}_0 = \Theta(n)$. So quantum buys nothing at all once error is forbidden -- against $\mathrm{Q} = \Theta(1)$.
SearchCNF relation \(\Theta(1)\) At most $\mathrm{R}_0 = \Theta(1)$; at least $\mathrm{Q}$.
Gap-Majority partial \(\Theta(n)\) putative Zero-error quantum is as expensive as determinism. If a $T$-query algorithm outputs $1$ with probability $p(x) \ge 1/2$ on $1$-inputs and $p(x) = 0$ on $0$-inputs, then $p$ is a non-negative polynomial of degree $\le 2T$, so its symmetrisation $\tilde p(t) = \mathbb{E}_{|x|=t}[p(x)]$ is a genuine univariate polynomial of degree $\le 2T$ vanishing at every integer $t \le n/2-\delta n$ -- that is $(1/2-\delta)n$ distinct roots -- and positive at $t \ge n/2+\delta n$. Hence $\mathrm{Q}_0 \ge (1/4-\delta/2)n$, and $\mathrm{Q}_0 \le \mathrm{R}_0 = \Theta(n)$. Against $\mathrm{Q} = \Theta(\min(n,1/\delta))$ this is an unbounded gap at constant $\delta$.
Gap-Majority at constant gap partial \(\Theta(n)\) putative The polynomial argument of \(\mathrm{GapMaj}^{\delta}_{n}\): a zero-error algorithm's acceptance polynomial symmetrises to a non-negative univariate with $\Theta(n)$ roots. Against $\mathrm{Q} = \Theta(1)$, unbounded.
Parity-labelled FIND-1 partial \(\Theta(1)\) $\mathrm{Q}_0 \le \mathrm{R}_0 = O(1)$; also $\mathrm{Q}_0 \le \mathrm{Q}_E = 1$.
Approximate index partial \(O(\sqrt{k\log k})\) At most $\mathrm{R}_0$; at least $\mathrm{Q}$.
Collision problem partial \(O(n)\) At most $\mathrm{R}_0 = n/2+1$; at least $\mathrm{Q} = \Omega(n^{1/3})$.
Element distinctness total \(\Theta(n)\) A zero-error algorithm is in particular a nondeterministic one for both $f$ and $\bar f$, so $\mathrm{Q}_0 \ge \mathrm{NQ} = \Theta(n)$; and $\mathrm{Q}_0 \le \mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{E}} = \Theta(n)$. So the $n^{2/3}$ walk really does need two-sided error: forbidding it costs the full $n^{1/3}$ factor.
Ambainis-Belovs function partial \(\Omega(n^{1/3})\) Between $\mathrm{Q} = \Omega(n^{1/3})$ and $\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{E}} = O(n)$.
Ben-David-Kundu total search problems relation \(\widetilde\Omega(\sqrt n)\) At least $\mathrm{Q}$.
Yamakawa-Zhandry problem relation \(\mathrm{poly}(n)\) putative Verifiability makes the quantum advantage zero-error: run the $\mathrm{poly}(n)$-query algorithm, spend $n$ more queries checking $H(i,y_i)=1$ for all $i$, and repeat on failure. So $\mathrm{Q}_0 = O(\mathrm{Q} + n) = \mathrm{poly}(n)$ against $\mathrm{R}_0 = 2^{\Omega(n)}$. Contrast \(\mathrm{SEARCH}_n\), where $\mathrm{Q}_0 = n$ because the all-zero input has no witness to verify; here totality is on the quantum side too.
Surjectivity total \(\tilde\Theta(n)\) Sandwiched: $\mathrm{Q}_{\varepsilon}(f) \le \mathrm{Q}_{0}(f) \le \mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{E}}(f)$, both $\tilde\Theta(n)$ here.
$k$-distinctness total \(\Omega\!\big(n^{3/4 - 1/(4(2^k-1))}\big),\ O(n)\) Only the generic $\mathrm{Q}_{\varepsilon} \le \mathrm{Q}_0 \le \mathrm{D}$. Zero error is not obviously free here, unlike on the search problems in this file: a $k$-collision found is a $k$-element certificate, but certifying that no symbol repeats $k$ times costs $\mathrm{C}^0 = n$, so a zero-error algorithm must be right on $0$-inputs with certainty.
Recursive Fourier sampling partial \(\Theta(2^{\ell}) \text{ for } g = g_{\mathrm{mod}3}\) Squeezed to nothing: $\mathrm{Q}_{\varepsilon} \le \mathrm{Q}_0 \le \mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{E}}$, and both ends are $\Theta(2^{\ell})$ here -- the exact algorithm already matches the bounded-error lower bound, so error tolerance buys nothing at all on $\mathrm{RFS}$.
Welded trees relation \(\Omega(n),\ O(n^{1.5}\log n)\) Sandwiched between $\mathrm{Q} = \Theta(n)$ and $\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{E}} = O(n^{1.5}\log n)$; closing it is the second of the two open questions in the notes below.
Local search on the hypercube relation \(\Theta(2^{n/3}n^{1/6})\) Same verification argument as $\mathrm{R}_0$: a candidate is checked with $\Delta = n$ further queries, so $\mathrm{Q}_0 = O(\mathrm{Q} + n)$, and $n \ll 2^{n/3}$. Zero error is free for every model on this problem, because the answer carries its own certificate.
Local search on the grid relation \(\Theta(k^{d/3})\ (d \ge 6)\) Same verification argument: $\mathrm{Q}_0 = O(\mathrm{Q} + d)$, and $d$ is constant. Zero error is free here for the same reason it is free classically.
Ordered search partial \(\Theta(\log n)\) Sandwiched: $\mathrm{Q}_{\varepsilon}(f) \le \mathrm{Q}_{0}(f) \le \mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{E}}(f)$, with both ends $\Theta(\log n)$ here.
Tseitin search on an expander relation \(O(\sqrt n)\) Zero error is free, as it is classically: a candidate vertex is checked with $d = O(1)$ further queries, so $\mathrm{Q}_0 = O(\mathrm{Q} + 1)$, and the $\mathrm{Q}$ row gives $O(\sqrt n)$.
Inner product mod 2 total \(\Theta(n)\) Sandwiched: $\mathrm{Q}_{\varepsilon}(f) \le \mathrm{Q}_{0}(f) \le \mathrm{D}(f)$, with $\mathrm{Q}_{\varepsilon}(f) = \Theta(n)$ and $\mathrm{D}(f) = n$ here.
Minsky-Papert one-in-a-box total \(\Omega(m^2),\ O(m^3)\) putative Lower: planting a $1$ in every box but one restricts $f$ to $\mathrm{OR}_{m^2}$, and zero-error quantum search has no speedup at all, $\mathrm{Q}_0(\mathrm{OR}_b) = b$ [BCWZ99]. Upper: $\mathrm{Q}_0 \le \mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{E}} = N$. The gap is genuine -- the zoo does not know whether zero-error costs the full $\Theta(N)$ here, though $\mathrm{Q} = \Theta(N^{1/2})$ shows bounded error does not. BCWZ99
Sink of a tournament total \(\Theta(m) = \Theta(\sqrt N)\) putative Zero-error quantum buys nothing here. Fix the tournament on $V\setminus\{v\}$ to be a sink-free one (any cyclic orientation, $m \ge 4$) and leave the $m-1$ edges at $v$ free: the restriction is exactly $\mathrm{AND}_{m-1}$, and zero-error quantum search has no speedup at all, $\mathrm{Q}_0(\mathrm{AND}_{m-1}) = m-1$ [BCWZ99]. Restrictions only decrease $\mathrm{Q}_0$, so $\mathrm{Q}_0 \ge m-1$; and $\mathrm{Q}_0 \le \mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{E}} = O(m)$. Note this pins $\mathrm{Q}_0$ even though the bounded-error $\mathrm{Q}$ is open between $\Omega(\sqrt m)$ and $O(m)$.
Scorpion graphs total \(\Theta(m) = \Theta(\sqrt N)\) putative Zero-error quantum buys nothing. Fix the triple $(s,t,b) = (1,2,3)$, make the feet pairwise non-adjacent, fix all touch pairs at their scorpion values except the $m-3$ body-foot pairs, and leave those free: for $m \ge 6$ the result is a scorpion iff all $m-3$ are present (no foot can take over as body, its degree being at most $1$), so the restriction is $\mathrm{AND}_{m-3}$. Zero-error quantum search has no speedup at all, $\mathrm{Q}_0(\mathrm{AND}_k) = k$ [BCWZ99], and restrictions only decrease $\mathrm{Q}_0$, so $\mathrm{Q}_0 \ge m-3$; and $\mathrm{Q}_0 \le \mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{E}} = O(m)$. BCWZ99
Graph connectivity total \(\Omega(m^{3/2}),\ O(m^2)\) Between $\mathrm{Q} = \Theta(m^{3/2})$ and $\mathrm{Q}_{\mathrm{E}} = O(m^2)$. Whether zero-error connectivity is as hard as exact connectivity is open as far as the zoo knows; note that the [DHHM06] algorithm is Grover-based and therefore genuinely bounded-error.
