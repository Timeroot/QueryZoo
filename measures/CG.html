\(\mathrm{CG}(f)\)

Certificate game complexity (private coin)

Inverse winning probability of a zero-communication game where two players must agree on a coordinate where their inputs differ.

Definition

Two players receive $x$ and $y$ with $f(x)\ne f(y)$ and, without communicating, must both output the same index $i$ with $x_i \ne y_i$. $\mathrm{CG}(f)$ is the inverse of the optimal (worst-case over $(x,y)$) winning probability, using private randomness.

Basic facts

  • $\mathrm{CG}^{\mathrm{ns}} \le \mathrm{CG}^{*} \le \mathrm{CG}^{\mathrm{pub}} \le \mathrm{CG}$. Private coin is the one variant of the four that escapes the collapse to the classical adversary bound [CGLMSG23].
  • The sandwich: $\max\{\mathrm{Adv}(f)^2,\ \mathrm{R}_0(f),\ \mathrm{CA}(f)\} \le \mathrm{CG}(f) \le \min\{\mathrm{C}^0(f)\,\mathrm{C}^1(f),\ O(\mathrm{EC}(f)^2)\}$ [CGLMSG23, Thm 5.10]. For total $f$. The definition itself is stated for possibly-partial $f$, but the sandwich is not: the certificate-product bound needs a $0$-certificate and a $1$-certificate to conflict, and on a promise problem the input stitched together from two non-conflicting certificates may simply be outside the domain. It fails badly there -- \(\mathrm{WT}_n\) has $\mathrm{C} = \Theta(1)$ and $\mathrm{R}_0 = 2^{\Omega(n)}$, so no bound of the form $\mathrm{R}_0 \le \mathrm{CG} \le \mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1$ can survive.
  • The upper bound $\mathrm{CG}(f) \le \mathrm{C}^0(f)\,\mathrm{C}^1(f)$ is one line: each player outputs a uniformly random index of a minimal certificate for their own input, and a $0$-certificate and a $1$-certificate must conflict somewhere. It is tight for $\mathrm{OR}$ and for $\oplus$.
  • $\mathrm{CG}$ is not bounded by $\mathrm{D}$: it can exceed the arity of the function. $\mathrm{CG}(\oplus_n) = \Theta(n^2)$, because $\mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1 = n^2$ above and $\mathrm{Adv}(\oplus_n)^2 = n^2$ below.
  • The single-bit private-coin variant (inputs at Hamming distance $1$) equals $\lambda(f)^2$ [CGLMSG23, Thm 8.9]; the single-bit public-coin variant is $\Theta(\mathrm{s}(f))$ [CGLMSG23, Thm 8.4]. Since restricting the adversary to distance-$1$ pairs can only help the players, these are lower bounds on the unrestricted games.

PARITY total \(\Theta(n^2)\) The headline case for this measure: above the arity. Both [CGLMSG23] bounds are tight on parity, and the result is quadratically larger than $\mathrm{D} = n$: private-coin certificate games are the one variant of the four that is not capped by decision-tree complexity. Cheat-sheet functions total \(\widetilde\Theta(n)\) putative Squeeze: $\mathrm{R}_0=O(\mathrm{CG})$ gives $\mathrm{CG}=\widetilde\Omega(n)$, and $\mathrm{CG}\le\mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1=\widetilde O(n)$ gives the matching upper bound.
Low-sensitivity function from unambiguous certificates total \(\widetilde\Omega(n^3)\) Squeeze: $\mathrm{R}_0=O(\mathrm{CG})$ against $\mathrm{R}_0=\widetilde\Omega(n^3)$.

Ambainis' exact-quantum function total \(\Omega(3^d)\) Squeeze: $\mathrm{CG}\ge\mathrm{CG}^{\mathrm{pub}}=\Theta(3^d)$, equally $\mathrm{R}_0=O(\mathrm{CG})$. Only a lower bound is recorded: the ceiling available is $\mathrm{CG}\le\mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1=\Theta(9^d)$ (or $\mathrm{CG}=O(\mathrm{EC}^2)$, the same), and private randomness is not bounded by $\mathrm{R}_0$ -- $\mathrm{CG}(\oplus_n)=\Theta(n^2)$ while $\mathrm{R}_0(\oplus_n)=n$.
FORRELATION partial \(O(n^{3/2})\) putative Private coins, so no shared index is available and the total-function ceiling $\mathrm{CG} \le \mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1$ is not licensed here. Both players output a uniformly random one of the $2n$ positions; since opposite-valued promise inputs differ in $\ge 0.29\sqrt n$ positions the win probability is $\ge 0.29\sqrt n/(2n)^2$, so $\mathrm{CG} = O(n^{3/2})$. From below $\mathrm{CG} \ge \mathrm{CG}^{\mathrm{pub}} = \Theta(\mathrm{CA})$, which is only $\Omega(1)$ here.
k-FORRELATION partial \(O(k^2n^2)\) putative Private coins: both players output a uniformly random one of the $kn$ positions and win whenever they collide on a position where the inputs differ, so the win probability is at least $1/(kn)^2$. The total-function ceiling $\mathrm{CG} \le \mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1$ is not available on a promise problem. From below only $\mathrm{CG} \ge \mathrm{CG}^{\mathrm{pub}}$.
Simon's problem partial \(O(2^n)\) putative Private coins. An injective $g$ and a two-to-one $g^{\prime}$ must differ on at least $2^{n-1}$ of the $2^n$ table entries: for each of the $2^{n-1}$ pairs $\{x, x\oplus s\}$ we have $g^{\prime}(x)=g^{\prime}(x\oplus s)$ but $g(x)\ne g(x\oplus s)$, so at least one of the two entries moves. Both players output a uniformly random entry, winning with probability $\ge 2^{n-1}/(2^n)^2$. Contrast $\mathrm{CG}^{\mathrm{pub}} = \Theta(1)$: this is another unbounded private-versus-public certificate-game gap, as on \(\mathrm{DJ}_n\).
Deutsch-Jozsa partial \(\Theta(N)\) putative The private-coin certificate game is linear here, and the argument is short. Upper: both players output a uniformly random index of $[N]$; when Alice holds a constant $x$ and Bob a balanced $y$ they differ on exactly $N/2$ indices, so they win with probability $(N/2)/N^2 = 1/(2N)$. Lower: fix Alice at $x = 0^N$, where she has a single output distribution $p$. Against a balanced $y$ her success is $\sum_{i \in \mathrm{supp}(y)} p(i)\,q_y(i) \le \max_{i \in \mathrm{supp}(y)} p(i)$. Let $y$ be the indicator of the $N/2$ indices with the smallest $p(i)$: the $(N/2)$-th largest value of $p$ is at most $2/N$, so the value is at most $2/N$ and $\mathrm{CG} \ge N/2$. Note the total-function sandwich $\mathrm{R}_0 \le \mathrm{CG} \le \mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1$ is not available on a promise problem and is not used. CGLMSG23
Bernstein-Vazirani partial \(\Omega(n),\ O(n^2)\) putative Two players hold the Hadamard codewords of distinct secrets $s \ne s^{\prime}$ and must agree on a position where the tables differ, with private coins only. Upper: both output a uniformly random one of the $n$ positions $e_1,\dots,e_n$; the tables differ at $e_j$ exactly when $s_j \ne s^{\prime}_j$, which happens for some $j$, so they win with probability $\ge 1/n^2$. Lower: fix the first player at $s$ with output distribution $p$ on $\{0,1\}^n$. The $n-1$ heaviest positions span a subspace of dimension $\le n-1$, so some $d \ne 0$ is orthogonal to all of them; against $s^{\prime} = s \oplus d$ the difference set is $\{i : \langle d,i\rangle = 1\}$, which misses all of them, and the win probability is at most the $n$-th largest value of $p$, hence $\le 1/n$. Against $\mathrm{CG}^{\mathrm{pub}} = \Theta(1)$ this is another unbounded private-versus-public gap, as on \(\mathrm{DJ}_n\).
Gap-Majority partial \(\Omega(n)\) putative Private coins, and unlike $\mathrm{CG}^{\mathrm{pub}} = \Theta(\min(n,1/\delta))$ it does not shrink with $\delta$. Upper: each player outputs a uniformly random position on which its own input is $1$ (resp. $0$); the two inputs differ on $\ge 2\delta n$ such positions, giving $\mathrm{CG} = O(n/\delta)$. Lower: fix the $1$-player at $x$ with output distribution $p$, and let the $0$-player hold $y$ obtained from $x$ by zeroing the $2\delta n$ positions of $\mathrm{supp}(x)$ carrying the least $p$-mass. The win probability is at most $\max_{i \in \Delta} p(i)$, and the $2\delta n$-th smallest of the $\ge n/2$ values $\{p(i) : x_i = 1\}$ is at most $2/n$. So $\mathrm{CG} \ge n/2$. At constant $\delta$ this is $\Theta(n)$ against $\mathrm{CG}^{\mathrm{pub}} = \Theta(1)$: private randomness is the one variant of the four that escapes the collapse to $\mathrm{CA}$.
Gap-Majority at constant gap partial \(\Theta(n)\) putative Private coins, and unlike $\mathrm{CG}^{\mathrm{pub}} = \Theta(1)$ it does not shrink with the gap: see the two-sided argument at \(\mathrm{GapMaj}^{\delta}_{n}\).
Approximate index partial \(O(k^2) = O(\log^2 n)\) putative A private-coin strategy, since the total-function sandwich $\mathrm{CG} \le \mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1$ is unavailable on a promise problem. Each player, with probability $1/2$, outputs a uniformly random one of the $k$ address bits, and otherwise outputs a random bit of the cell indexed by its own address $a$. If the two addresses differ, they agree on a differing address bit with probability $\ge 1/(4k^2)$; if they are equal, they both name the same cell, which must differ, with probability $\Omega(1)$. So $\mathrm{CG} = O(k^2)$, while $\mathrm{CG}^{\mathrm{pub}} = O(\sqrt{k\log k})$. CGLMSG23
Collision problem partial \(O(n)\) putative Private coins: both players output a uniformly random position, winning with probability $|\Delta|/n^2 \ge 1/(2n)$. The total-function sandwich $\mathrm{CG} \le \mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1 = O(n)$ happens to give the same bound but is not available on a promise problem, so the strategy is the proof. From below only $\Omega(\mathrm{CG}^{\mathrm{pub}}) = \Omega(1)$. CGLMSG23
Element distinctness total \(\Theta(n)\) The sandwich $\max\{\mathrm{Adv}^2, \mathrm{R}_0, \mathrm{CA}\} \le \mathrm{CG} \le \min\{\mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1, O(\mathrm{EC}^2)\}$ needs a total function, and this one is total: $\mathrm{R}_0 = \Theta(n)$ below and $\mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1 = 2n$ above. Note how tight the certificate-product bound is here precisely because the two sides are lopsided -- compare \(\mathrm{forr}\), where the same product is worthless. CGLMSG23
Ambainis-Belovs function partial \(O(n^2)\) putative Private coins: both players output a uniformly random one of the $3n$ positions, winning with probability $\ge 1/(3n)^2$. The sandwich $\max\{\mathrm{Adv}^2,\mathrm{R}_0,\mathrm{CA}\} \le \mathrm{CG} \le \min\{\mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1, O(\mathrm{EC}^2)\}$ is a total-function statement and is not used here.
Surjectivity total \(\Omega(n),\ O(n^2/\log n)\) The old reading $\mathrm{bs} \le \mathrm{CG} \le \mathrm{D}$ was wrong on the right: $\mathrm{CG}$ is not capped by the arity. The correct sandwich is $\max\{\mathrm{Adv}^2, \mathrm{R}_0, \mathrm{CA}\} \le \mathrm{CG} \le \mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1$ [CGLMSG23]. Here $\mathrm{R}_0 = \Theta(n)$ from below, and $\mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1 = \Theta(n/\log n)\cdot\Theta(n)$ from above. The $\mathrm{Adv}$ route gives nothing extra, since only $\mathrm{Adv} = O(n/\sqrt{\log n})$ is recorded. The gap is genuine and left open. CGLMSG23
$k$-distinctness total \(\Theta(n)\) For constant $k$ the correct sandwich $\mathrm{R}_0 \le \mathrm{CG} \le \mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1$ [CGLMSG23] closes: $\mathrm{R}_0 = \Theta(n)$ and $\mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1 = n \cdot k = \Theta(n)$. The value happens to agree with the one previously recorded, but the old justification -- $\mathrm{CG} \le \mathrm{D}$ -- is false in general: $\mathrm{CG}$ can exceed the arity, as it does for $\oplus_n$. For growing $k$ the ceiling degrades to $O(nk)$. CGLMSG23
Triangle finding total \(\Theta(m^2) = \Theta(n)\) Pinned by $\mathrm{R}_0 \le \mathrm{CG} \le \mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1$ [CGLMSG23]: a $1$-certificate is a single triangle, so $\mathrm{C}^1 = 3$ and the product is $\binom m2 \cdot 3 = \Theta(m^2)$, matching $\mathrm{R}_0 = \Theta(m^2)$ from below. Consistent with $\mathrm{Adv}^2 = \Theta(m^2)$. The lopsided certificate sizes are what make this one of the rare cases where $\mathrm{CG}$ stays at the arity; the old note justified it by the false $\mathrm{CG} \le \mathrm{D}$. CGLMSG23
Inner product mod 2 total \(\Theta(n^2)\) putative Sandwiched: $\mathrm{Adv}(f)^2 \le \mathrm{CG}(f) \le \mathrm{C}^0(f)\,\mathrm{C}^1(f)$ [CGLMSG23], with $\mathrm{Adv} = \Theta(n)$ and $\mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1 = \Theta(n^2)$ here. Note that this is above the arity: the private-coin game is the one certificate-game variant that is not bounded by $\mathrm{D}$. Inner product behaves exactly like \(\oplus_n\) here, and for the same reason -- distance-$1$ pairs force both players to guess the same index out of $\Theta(n)$ candidates independently. CGLMSG23
Minsky-Papert one-in-a-box total \(\Theta(m^3) = \Theta(N)\) Pinned by the total-function sandwich $\max\{\mathrm{Adv}^2,\mathrm{R}_0,\mathrm{CA}\} \le \mathrm{CG} \le \mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1$ [CGLMSG23]: the bottom is $\mathrm{Adv}^2 = \Theta(m^3)$ (equally $\mathrm{R}_0 = \Theta(m^3)$) and the top is $\mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1 = m^2\cdot m = m^3$. So the private-coin game costs the full $\mathrm{D}$ here, a whole $N^{1/3}$ above the public-coin value $\Theta(m^2)$ -- the largest separation between the two variants that the $\mathrm{CG} \le \mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1$ ceiling permits on this function. CGLMSG23
Sink of a tournament total \(\Omega(m),\ O(m^2)\) The sandwich is $\max\{\mathrm{Adv}^2, \mathrm{R}_0\} \le \mathrm{CG} \le \mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1$ [CGLMSG23], which here reads $\max\{\Omega(m), \Theta(m)\} \le \mathrm{CG} \le m(m-1)$. It closes to $\Theta(m)$ exactly if $\mathrm{Adv} = \Theta(\sqrt m)$ and to $\Theta(m^2)$ if $\mathrm{Adv} = \Theta(m)$ -- the same open question as the \(\mathrm{Q}_{\varepsilon}(f)\) row of this entry. The old reading $\mathrm{CG} \le \mathrm{D}$ is not available: $\mathrm{CG}$ is not bounded by the arity. CGLMSG23
Scorpion graphs total \(\Omega(m),\ O(m^2)\) The sandwich is $\max\{\mathrm{Adv}^2, \mathrm{R}_0, \mathrm{CA}\} \le \mathrm{CG} \le \mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1$ [CGLMSG23]: here $\mathrm{R}_0 = \Theta(m)$ from below and $\mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1 = \Theta(m)\cdot(3m-6) = \Theta(m^2)$ from above, and $\mathrm{Adv}$ is itself only pinned to $[\Omega(\sqrt m), O(m)]$. Note the direction: $\mathrm{R}_0 = O(\mathrm{CG})$, not the reverse -- private coin is the one variant of the game that is harder than zero-error query complexity, and is not capped by $\mathrm{D}$. CGLMSG23
Graph connectivity total \(\Theta(m^3) = \Theta(N^{3/2})\) putative Both ends of the [CGLMSG23] sandwich land on $m^3$: $\mathrm{Adv}(f)^2 = \Theta(m^{3/2})^2 = \Theta(m^3)$ from below, and $\mathrm{C}^0\mathrm{C}^1 = \Theta(m^2)\cdot(m-1) = \Theta(m^3)$ from above. So connectivity is one of the sharpest examples in the zoo of $\mathrm{CG}$ exceeding the arity -- $\mathrm{CG} = \Theta(N^{3/2})$ on $N = \binom{m}{2}$ variables. CGLMSG23
