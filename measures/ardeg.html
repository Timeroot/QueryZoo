\(\mathrm{rdeg}_{\varepsilon}(f)\)

Approximate rational degree

Bounded-error version of rational degree; equals post-selected bounded-error quantum query complexity.

Definition

The $\varepsilon>0$ case of \(\mathrm{rdeg}(f)\); $\mathrm{rdeg}_{1/3}$ is the standard choice.

Parameters

\(\varepsilon\) (eps) default \(1/3\)

Basic facts

  • $\mathrm{PostQ}_\varepsilon(f)=\Theta(\mathrm{rdeg}_\varepsilon(f))$ [MahadevdeWolf15].
  • $\mathrm{rdeg}_\varepsilon(f)\le\mathrm{rdeg}(f)$ and $\mathrm{rdeg}_\varepsilon(f)\le\widetilde{\deg}_\varepsilon(f)$.

Equivalent / near-equivalent measures

  • \(\mathrm{PostQ}_{\varepsilon}(f)\) $\mathrm{rdeg}_\varepsilon(f) = \Theta(\mathrm{PostQ}_\varepsilon(f))$ for constant $\varepsilon$ MahadevdeWolf15 IJKKKSWW23

OR total \(1\) The single rational function $r(x) = \dfrac{n\sum_i x_i}{1 + n\sum_i x_i}$ has numerator and denominator of degree $1$, is $0$ at $0^n$, and is $\ge \tfrac{n}{n+1} \ge \tfrac23$ at every $1$-input. So one degree of freedom suffices where $\widetilde{\deg} = \Theta(\sqrt n)$ is needed for polynomials -- the cleanest illustration that rational approximation is a strictly different resource. PARITY total \(n\) Rational approximation does not help at all here. Suppose $p/q$ approximates $\mathrm{PARITY}$ to within $1/3$ with $\deg p, \deg q \le d$ and $q > 0$ on the cube. Symmetrising over the slices, $P(k) - \tfrac12 Q(k)$ is a univariate polynomial of degree $\le d$ whose sign alternates at $k = 0,1,\dots,n$, so it has $\ge n$ roots and $d \ge n$. EXACT-HALF total \(O(1)\) putative Degree $2$ suffices: $r(x) = \big(1 + 2(\sum_i x_i - n/2)^2\big)^{-1}$ equals $1$ on the middle slice and is at most $1/3$ everywhere else, since $|\sum_i x_i - n/2| \ge 1$ off it. This is the sharpest gap in the zoo between rational and polynomial approximation: $\widetilde{\deg} = \Theta(n)$ and even $\mathrm{rdeg} = \Theta(n)$ (exact rational representation), yet approximate rational degree is constant. ODD-MAX-BIT total \(1\) putative Degree $1$ suffices, as for \(\mathrm{OR}_n\). Take $N(x) = \sum_{i \text{ odd}} 10^i x_i$ and $D(x) = 1 + \sum_i 10^i x_i$, both of degree $1$, and let $h$ be the highest position with $x_h = 1$ (with $h=0$ for $x = 0^n$, where $N/D = 0$). Then $10^h \le D \le 1 + \tfrac{10}{9}(10^h-1) < 1.112\cdot 10^h$. If $h$ is odd then $10^h \le N \le \tfrac{100}{99}10^h$, so $N/D \ge 0.899$; if $h$ is even then $N \le \tfrac{100}{99}10^{h-1}$, so $N/D \le 0.102$. Rational approximation famously does not compose -- the outer rational function is evaluated at perturbed inputs where its denominator can vanish -- so the $O(\log)$ rational approximations of the individual $\mathrm{AND}$ and $\mathrm{OR}$ gates give nothing for the tree.
