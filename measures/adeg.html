\(\widetilde{\deg}_{\varepsilon}(f)\)

Approximate degree

Least degree of a polynomial that approximates f pointwise to within eps.

also written: deg~, eps-approximate degree

Definition

$\widetilde{\deg}_\varepsilon(f)$ is the least degree of a real polynomial $p$ with $|p(x)-f(x)|\le\varepsilon$ for all $x\in\mathrm{Dom}(f)$ and (for partial $f$) $|p(x)|\le 1+\varepsilon$ for all $x\in\{0,1\}^n$. Unadorned $\widetilde{\deg}(f):=\widetilde{\deg}_{1/3}(f)$.

Parameters

\(\varepsilon\) (eps) default \(1/3\)range \([0, 1/2)\)

  • Error reduction: $\widetilde{\deg}_{\varepsilon}(f)=O(\widetilde{\deg}_{1/3}(f)\log(1/\varepsilon))$ (compose with an amplifying univariate polynomial).
  • $\varepsilon\to 0$: $\widetilde{\deg}_0(f)=\deg(f)$, and $\deg(f)=O(\widetilde{\deg}(f)^2)$, so the whole range $\varepsilon\in(0,1/3]$ spans at most a quadratic factor for total $f$.
  • Large error / small bias: $\widetilde{\deg}_{1/2-\gamma}(f)$ can be far smaller; at $\gamma\to 0$ it degenerates towards sign degree \(\mathrm{sdeg}(f)\). The "large-error approximate degree" $\widetilde{\deg}_{1/2-1/\mathrm{poly}}$ is its own subject with applications to $\mathsf{PP}$ and to communication complexity.

Detailed records: \(\widetilde{\deg}_{\varepsilon}(f) = O\big(\widetilde{\deg}_{1/3}(f)\log(1/\varepsilon)\big)\) · \(\widetilde{\deg}_{\varepsilon}(f) \text{ as } \varepsilon \to 0 \text{ and } \varepsilon\to 1/2\)

Basic facts

  • $\widetilde{\deg}(f) \le 2\,\mathrm{Q}(f)$: the polynomial method [BBCMdW01].
  • $\mathrm{D}(f) = O(\widetilde{\deg}(f)^4)$ [ABKRT21], improving $O(\widetilde{\deg}^6)$ [BBCMdW01]; matching separation $\mathrm{D}=\tilde\Omega(\widetilde{\deg}^4)$ [ABBLSS17]. Exponent settled at 4.
  • $\mathrm{bs}(f)\le 6\widetilde{\deg}(f)^2$ [Nisan-Szegedy]; $\mathrm{RC}(f)=O(\widetilde{\deg}(f)^2)$ [Kulkarni-Tal].
  • Paturi: for symmetric $f$, $\widetilde{\deg}(f)=\Theta(\sqrt{n(n-\Gamma(f))})$ where $\Gamma(f)=\min\{|2k-n+1| : f_k\ne f_{k+1}\}$.
  • Read-once formulas: $\widetilde{\deg}(f)=\Theta(\sqrt n)$ [ABKRT21, Thm 6].
  • $\lambda(f)=O(\widetilde{\deg}(f))$.
  • For total Boolean $f,g$, $\widetilde{\deg}(f\circ g)=\Theta(\widetilde{\deg}(f)\widetilde{\deg}(g))$: composition holds up to constants [PednekarPodder26composition, Sherstov12robust].

Marked bit partial \(\Theta(\sqrt n)\) putative The value the function exists for. Upper: $\widetilde{\deg} \le 2\mathrm{Q}$. Lower, by reduction to $\mathrm{OR}$ rather than by symmetrising: let $p$ be $\varepsilon$-approximating and $[0,1]$-valued on the whole cube, and set $P(u) = p(y=u, z=0)$ and $W(u) = p(y=0, z=u)$, both of degree $\le \deg p$. For $u \ne 0^n$ the input $(u,0)$ is a domain $1$-input and $(0,u)$ a domain $0$-input, so $P(u) \ge 1-\varepsilon$ and $W(u) \le \varepsilon$; and $P(0^n) = W(0^n) = p(0,0)$, the all-blank string, whatever that unconstrained value is. So $\Psi = P - W$ has $\Psi(0^n) = 0$, $\Psi(u) \ge 1-2\varepsilon$ off the origin, and $|\Psi| \le 1$ everywhere. So $\Psi$ is a $[-1,1]$-bounded polynomial on the cube separating $0^n$ from the rest by a constant gap ($0$ versus $\ge 1/3$ at $\varepsilon = 1/3$), which is exactly what the Nisan--Szegedy argument rules out below degree $\Omega(\sqrt n)$: symmetrise $\Psi$, and Ehlich--Zeller plus Markov give $\deg p \ge \deg \Psi = \Omega(\sqrt n)$ [NS94]. The cancellation at the origin is the point -- neither $P$ nor $W$ alone is pinned there, but their difference is. NS94 BBCMdW01

Approximate index partial \(O(\sqrt{k\log k})\) $\widetilde{\deg} \le 2\mathrm{Q} \le 2\mathrm{R} = O(\sqrt{k\log k})$. From below, only $\Omega(1)$, and closing that is a real question rather than an oversight -- note that $\deg = 2$ below is no help, because approximate degree is the one polynomial measure that insists on boundedness off the promise. What a bounded polynomial can do here has a clean reading. Suppose $c_b(a)$, $b \in \{0,1\}^k$, are polynomials in the address of degree $\le d$ with $c_b \ge 0$ and $\sum_b c_b(a) \le 1$ on the whole cube, and $\sum_{b \in B(a,r)} c_b(a) \ge 2/3$ for every $a$ -- a guessing scheme, a distribution over cells of low degree in $a$ that lands in the ball. Then $p(a,x) = \sum_b c_b(a)\big(\mathbb{1}[x_b=1] - \mathbb{1}[x_b=0]\big)$ has degree $d+2$, satisfies $|p| \le 1$ everywhere because the $c_b$ are nonnegative and sum to at most $1$, and equals $(2v-1)\sum_{b \in B(a,r)} c_b(a)$ on the promise; so $\widetilde{\deg} \le d+2$. Two schemes are available and neither beats the algorithm: reading a prefix of $8\sqrt{k\log k}$ address bits and guessing the rest uniformly has degree $8\sqrt{k\log k}$, and biasing every bit by $\gamma = c\sqrt{\log k/k}$ -- the \(\mathrm{noisyR}(f)\) strategy -- gives $c_b(a) = \prod_i (\tfrac12 + \gamma(1-2b_i)(1-2a_i))$, degree $k$, which is worse. Whether some cleverer nonnegative scheme, or a polynomial not of this shape at all, does better is open.
Collision problem partial \(\Theta(n^{1/3})\) The [AaronsonShi04]/[Kutin05] lower bound is a degree bound -- that is what the polynomial method proves -- so $\widetilde{\deg} = \Omega(n^{1/3})$; and $\widetilde{\deg} \le 2\mathrm{Q} = O(n^{1/3})$. Approximate degree is thus the tight measure for the collision problem, where $\mathrm{Adv}$ collapses to $O(1)$. AaronsonShi04 Kutin05
Element distinctness total \(\Theta(n^{2/3})\) Upper: $\widetilde{\deg} \le 2\mathrm{Q} = O(n^{2/3})$ by the polynomial method applied to the walk of [Ambainis07]. Lower: the $\Omega(n^{2/3})$ bound of [AaronsonShi04] is a degree bound -- it is proved by symmetrising a low-degree approximating polynomial -- and [Ambainis05smallrange] brings it down to alphabet size $n$. So $\widetilde{\deg}$ is the tight measure here while $\deg = n$. AaronsonShi04 Ambainis05smallrange Ambainis07

Triangle finding total \(\Omega(m),\ O(m^{5/4})\) Open, and open in exactly the same window as $\mathrm{Q}$. Lower: $\widetilde{\deg} = \Omega(\sqrt{\mathrm{bs}}) = \Omega(m)$ by Nisan-Szegedy, which is all that is known -- the Bun-Kothari-Thaler machinery that settled $\widetilde{\deg}$ for \(\mathrm{SURJ}_{N,R}\) and pushed \(\mathrm{DIST}^k_n\) has not been made to say anything here.
