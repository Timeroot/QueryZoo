Composition

Does M(f o g) = M(f) M(g)? The answer distinguishes the well-behaved measures from the rest, and has spawned a family of measures of its own.

For $f$ on $n$ bits and $g$ on $m$ bits, $f\circ g^n$ is the function on $nm$ bits obtained by feeding $n$ disjoint copies of $g$ into $f$.

Why $\mathrm{R}$ is hard

The natural algorithm for $f\circ g^n$ runs the outer algorithm and amplifies each inner call to error $1/(3\mathrm{R}(f))$, costing $O(\mathrm{R}(f)\mathrm{R}(g)\log \mathrm{R}(f))$. Two things can go wrong with the matching lower bound:

  1. The outer algorithm may tolerate noisy answers from the inner copies, paying less than full price per copy. This is what \(\mathrm{noisyR}(f)\) measures, and $\mathrm{R}(f\circ g^n)=\Omega(\mathrm{noisyR}(f)\mathrm{R}(g))$ is the optimal theorem of that shape.
  2. The outer algorithm may not need a decision from each inner copy, only partial information. This is what \(\chi(f)\), \(\bar{\chi}(f)\), \(\mathrm{RS}(f)\) and \(\mathrm{LR}(f)\) measure, and $\mathrm{R}(f\circ g^n)=\Omega(\mathrm{R}(f)\mathrm{LR}(g))$ is the optimal theorem of that shape.

Neither optimal theorem implies the conjecture, and for partial functions the conjecture is simply false.

The inner hierarchy

$$\chi \;\lesssim\; \mathrm{RS} \;\lesssim\; \bar\chi \;\lesssim\; \mathrm{LR} \;\lesssim\; \mathrm{R}, \qquad \mathrm{LR} = \Omega(\sqrt{\mathrm{R}}).$$ All known separations in this chain use partial functions. For total functions no separation is known anywhere in it -- including between $\mathrm{RS}$ and $\mathrm{R}$, which is Is … for total …?. The easiest case of composition is an identity outer function, i.e. simply solving many copies -- see Direct sum and direct product, where even that is delicate for $\mathrm{R}$.
