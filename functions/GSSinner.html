\(g_n\)

Random-indicator gadget

The inner gadget of \(f_{\mathrm{GSS}}\), on its own: $\mathrm{C}^0 = \Theta(n)$ with $\mathrm{bs}^0 = O(1)$.

total separating arity: $n$ bits— also: the GSS inner function, indicator of a random code

Description

The indicator of $2^{n/50}$ points of the cube chosen uniformly at random. A random set that sparse is a good code -- its points are far apart, so no $0$-input has more than a constant number of disjoint sensitive blocks -- and yet it is dense enough that no small subcube avoids it, so every $0$-input needs a linear certificate.

Formal definition

Pick $x^1,\dots,x^N \in \{0,1\}^n$ uniformly at random with $N = 2^{n/50}$ and let $g_n$ be their indicator. With high probability [GSS16, Thm 32, Claim 34]:

  • the points are at pairwise Hamming distance $\ge n/100$, so a $0$-input has at most $200$ disjoint sensitive blocks -- $\mathrm{bs}^0(g_n),\ \mathrm{fbs}^0(g_n) = O(1)$;
  • every subcube of codimension $n/100$ contains one of the points, so no $0$-certificate is shorter than $n/100$ -- $\mathrm{C}^0(g_n)\ge n/100$.

The $1$-side is the opposite extreme: a $1$-input is an isolated point, so all $n$ of its bits are sensitive and its only certificate is the whole input.

Notes

Recorded separately from \(f_{\mathrm{GSS}}\) because the two say different things. The composed $\mathrm{OR}_n\circ g_n$ multiplies the $0$-side by $n$ and so turns a constant $\mathrm{bs}^0$ into $\mathrm{bs}^0 = \Theta(n)$; that is what makes it a quadratic $\mathrm{C}$-vs-$\mathrm{bs}$ separation. The gadget on its own is the extreme case in a different direction: the ratio $\mathrm{C}^0/\mathrm{bs}^0$ is unbounded, which no amount of composition can improve on and which the composed function hides.

Not explicit: the construction is probabilistic, and the properties hold with high probability over the choice of the $N$ points. That is enough for a separation -- some choice works -- but the family has no description shorter than the list of points.
