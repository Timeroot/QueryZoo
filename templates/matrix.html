Reading a cell

Entry \(a,b\) in row \(M_1\), column \(M_2\): \(a\) is the best separation exponent (some \(g\) has \(M_1(g)\ge M_2(g)^{a-o(1)}\)); \(b\) is the best upper bound exponent (all \(f\) in the setting have \(M_1(f)\le M_2(f)^{b+o(1)}\)). A ? on either side means the zoo knows of nothing there.

The chip: what the exponent hides

An exponent is only defined up to \(n^{o(1)}\), so \(1,1\) can mean anything from a one-line inequality to a theorem with polylog slack. The superscript chip says which:

  • ≤ exact — \(M_1(f) \le M_2(f)^b\) on the nose, no hidden constant
  • Θ theta — \(M_1 = \Theta(M_2^{b})\): tight up to constants, in both directions
  • Õ tilde — \(M_1 = \widetilde O(M_2^{b})\): tight up to polylog factors
  • O constant — \(M_1 = O(M_2^{b})\), the constant being explicit on the pair page where it is known
  •   no chip — only the exponent is on record, with the \(o(1)\) unquantified
  • = attained — the upper bound is exact and a family meets it with equality. The pair is settled with nothing left to shave, not even a constant: \(\mathrm{D}\) vs \(\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{na}}\) reads 1,1= because \(\mathrm{D}\le\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{na}}\) always and \(\oplus_n\) has \(\mathrm{D}=\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{na}}=n\).

So \(\mathrm{QC}\) vs \(\mathrm{RC}\) reads 0.5,0.5Θ: \(\mathrm{QC} = \Theta(\sqrt{\mathrm{RC}})\), settled and tight to a constant.

Derived cells

Cells with a 1,2 dotted underline are not in the data: the build computed them, and the tooltip says how. Three rules do the work —

  • transitive closure: \(M_1 \le M_2^{p}\) and \(M_2 \le M_3^{q}\) give \(M_1 = O(M_3^{pq})\), carrying log factors and tightness along;
  • separations pushed forward: a family with \(M_1 \ge M_2^{p}\), plus a universal \(M_3 = O(M_2^{r})\), separates \(M_1\) from \(M_3\) at exponent \(p/r\);
  • setting inheritance: an upper bound proved for relations holds for partial and total functions; a separation exhibited by a total function is a separation for partial functions and for relations. See {{ xref('settings') }}.

Nothing derived is ever written back to the YAML, and a recorded statement always wins over a derived one of the same strength. hide derived cells blanks them, leaving exactly what a human wrote down.

Cells with no exponent

  • ⊘ proved that no bound of this form exists in this setting — the usual fate of a polynomial relation on partial functions
  • ∞ unbounded: \(M_1\) can be arbitrarily large at fixed \(M_2\)
  • exp / log in place of an exponent — the relationship is real but not polynomial. A cell reading exp,exp is as settled as \(2,2\) is: \(\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{na}}\) vs \(\mathrm{D}\) has \(\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{na}} \le 2^{\mathrm{D}}-1\) and a family attaining it. A single side reading exp means only that side is known.
  • ∘ on the diagonal: records about a measure and itself — composition theorems, \(\varepsilon\)-dependence
  • blank — nothing recorded and nothing derivable for that ordered pair

Views and scope

Total / Partial / Relations switches which setting each cell reports; every exponent, chip and colour is recomputed for that setting, so most of the grid goes pale when you leave the total world — that is the honest picture.

Core is the published table. With records adds every measure appearing in at least one relation record. All shows all {{ meta.counts.all }} measures in the zoo, including ones whose pairs are entirely unpopulated — useful for seeing what is missing. Narrow either with the category chips.

Blank means the zoo has no entry for that pair in that setting yet — it does not mean nothing is known; see {{ xref('settings') }}.
