The chip: what the exponent hides

An exponent is only defined up to \(n^{o(1)}\), so \(1,1\) can mean anything from a one-line inequality to a theorem with polylog slack. The superscript chip says which:

  • ≤ exact — \(M_1(f) \le M_2(f)^b\) on the nose, no hidden constant
  • Θ theta — \(M_1 = \Theta(M_2^{b})\): tight up to constants, in both directions
  • Õ tilde — \(M_1 = \widetilde O(M_2^{b})\): tight up to polylog factors
  • O constant — \(M_1 = O(M_2^{b})\), the constant being explicit on the pair page where it is known
  •   no chip — only the exponent is on record, with the \(o(1)\) unquantified
  • = attained — the upper bound is exact and a family meets it with equality. The pair is settled with nothing left to shave, not even a constant: \(\mathrm{D}\) vs \(\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{na}}\) reads 1,1= because \(\mathrm{D}\le\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{na}}\) always and \(\oplus_n\) has \(\mathrm{D}=\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{na}}=n\).

So \(\mathrm{QC}\) vs \(\mathrm{RC}\) reads 0.5,0.5Θ: \(\mathrm{QC} = \Theta(\sqrt{\mathrm{RC}})\), settled and tight to a constant.

Derived cells

Cells with a 1,2 dotted underline are not in the data: the build computed them, and the tooltip says how. Three rules do the work —

  • transitive closure: \(M_1 \le M_2^{p}\) and \(M_2 \le M_3^{q}\) give \(M_1 = O(M_3^{pq})\), carrying log factors and tightness along;
  • separations pushed forward: a family with \(M_1 \ge M_2^{p}\), plus a universal \(M_3 = O(M_2^{r})\), separates \(M_1\) from \(M_3\) at exponent \(p/r\); the same family and a universal \(M_1 = O(M_3^{r})\) instead separate \(M_3\) from \(M_2\), since \(M_3\) is then polynomially larger than \(M_1\);
  • setting inheritance: an upper bound proved for relations holds for partial and total functions; a separation exhibited by a total function is a separation for partial functions and for relations. See {{ xref('settings') }}.

Nothing derived is ever written back to the YAML, and a recorded statement always wins over a derived one of the same strength. hide derived cells blanks them, leaving exactly what a human wrote down.

Cells with no exponent

  • ∞ proved that no bound exists in this setting: \(M_1\) is not \(O(M_2^k)\) for any \(k\) — the usual fate of a polynomial relation on partial functions. Usually \(M_1\) can be made arbitrarily large at fixed \(M_2\); among the normalized quantities, where neither side can grow, it is the ratio that is unbounded because the denominator collapses.
  • exp / log in place of an exponent — the relationship is real but not polynomial. A cell reading exp,exp is as settled as \(2,2\) is: \(\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{na}}\) vs \(\mathrm{D}\) has \(\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{na}} \le 2^{\mathrm{D}}-1\) and a family attaining it. A single side reading exp means only that side is known.
  • ∘ on the diagonal: records about a measure and itself — composition theorems, \(\varepsilon\)-dependence
  • blank — nothing recorded and nothing derivable for that ordered pair
  • blank in a row or column whose header carries a ° — not a gap: the measure is only partially defined in the setting you are looking at, and the zoo propagates nothing into or out of a setting where a measure is not squarely defined. Hover the marker for which part of the definition is missing.

Views and scope

Total / Partial / Relations switches which setting each cell reports; every exponent, chip and colour is recomputed for that setting, so most of the grid goes pale when you leave the total world — that is the honest picture.

Some measures are left out of every grid, and the count line says how many. A measure with no conventional definition in the setting on screen has nothing to report there (\(\mathrm{MaxInf}\) for relations). Neither does one that degenerates in that setting: \(\mathrm{cbs} = \mathrm{bs}\) exactly on functions, and \(\mathrm{psD} = \Theta(\mathrm{R})\) once the answer is unique, so their rows would only restate another row. Nor do the dimensionless ones — \(\mathrm{Var}\), \(\mathrm{MaxInf}\), \(\mathrm{NS}_\delta\), \(\mathrm{Stab}_\rho\) live in \([0,1]\), so no exponent relates them to a query count, in any setting. All of them keep their measure and pair pages, and their relations are still computed and closed over: that is where the collapses are recorded, and they double as a check on the closure.

The dimensionless quantities do appear in the grid, in normalized form: what has a query scale is not the influence but the ratio \(\mathrm{osss} = \mathrm{Var}/\mathrm{MaxInf}\), the quantity the OSSS inequality bounds by the average depth of a decision tree, and not the noise sensitivity but the critical rate, \(\mathrm{nsr} = 1/\delta^{*}\). Each of the four hidden pages explains its own normalization; the concept page gives the general recipe.

Core is the classical/quantum core: the thirteen measures the exponent table records, in its row order. \(\mathrm{rdeg}\) carries a column but no recorded row, because it sits at the bottom of the order and the row is all corollary — every upper bound in it factors through \(\mathrm{rdeg}\le\mathrm{C}\) and every separation is witnessed by \(\vee_n\). It is drawn anyway, from the derived layer, and marked †. With records adds every measure appearing in at least one relation record. All adds the rest of the {{ meta.counts.all }} measures in the zoo, including ones whose pairs are entirely unpopulated — useful for seeing what is missing. Narrow either with the category chips.

The number on a scope button is how many measures it draws, in the setting on screen and under the chips showing — not how many exist, since the rows hidden for the reasons above never appear in any of them. Where two scopes would draw the same grid the narrower button is dropped rather than left to look broken: as the zoo stands the only measure with no relation record is a dimensionless one, which is hidden anyway, so With records and All coincide and only All is offered.

Ladder drops the grouping by subject and puts {{ meta.counts.ladder }} of the measures in a rough order of size, biggest first, so the direction of the whole table is visible by eye: the free \(1,1^{\le}\) inequalities collect below the diagonal, where the row measure is the smaller one, and the real exponents and separations sit above it. {{ site.ladder_note | mdi }}

Blank means the zoo has no entry for that pair in that setting yet — it does not mean nothing is known; see {{ xref('settings') }}.
